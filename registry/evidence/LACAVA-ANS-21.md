## [LACAVA-ANS-21] Understanding the growth of the Fediverse through the lens of Mastodon
**Citation:** Lucio La Cava, Sergio Greco, Andrea Tagarelli. "Understanding the growth of the Fediverse through the lens of Mastodon." Applied Network Science 6, 2021. DOI 10.1007/S41109-021-00392-5.
**Retrieved:** full text via https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15473
**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15473
**Domain:** J

### What it does
The paper builds an up-to-date crawled dataset of the Mastodon instance-to-instance follow network and uses it to measure the topological structure of that network and how the structure changed over roughly three years, compared against an earlier published Mastodon dataset. It does not measure the distribution of user accounts across instances (how many users each instance hosts); the measured object throughout is a directed graph whose nodes are Mastodon server instances and whose edges are aggregated follow relationships between users of one instance and users of another.

Data was collected by a purpose-built crawler using only the public, authenticated Mastodon REST API, starting from a seed list of instances obtained from the instances.social tracker (approximately 900 of about 1,100 candidate instances granted the crawler an authentication token). From the timelines of these seed instances the crawler discovered approximately 81,000 seed users, then performed a breadth-first search over the user-follow graph, discovering new users and instances as it traversed. Collected edges were anonymized by hashing at collection time, so the raw dataset cannot be traced back to individual identities; no scraping outside the authenticated API was performed, so instances that declined to grant an authentication token contribute no data beyond what other instances' users reveal about them.

Three network models are derived from the same underlying crawled user-follow graph, all as directed graphs of instances rather than of individual users: the Instances network (edges exist between two instances if any user of one follows any user of the other, restricted to instances known to be Mastodon), the Online-Instances network (the subgraph restricted to instances currently reported online by the tracker sites), and the Expanded-Instances network (the same graph but including edges to and from instances belonging to other Fediverse platforms besides Mastodon, discovered incidentally because Mastodon's follow-listing API does not distinguish the platform of a followed or following account).

Instance status (Mastodon versus another platform, online versus offline) is determined by cross-referencing the crawled instance identifiers against two independent third-party tracker websites, instances.social and fediverse.party, rather than being inferred from the crawl itself.

The paper reports negative degree assortativity (disassortative mixing, i.e., high-degree instances tend to connect to low-degree instances rather than to each other) as a consistent feature across every network variant tested (Instances: -0.253 to -0.274 depending on edge-direction treatment; Online-Instances: -0.284 to -0.290; the three-years-earlier network: -0.287 to -0.291), and states this distinguishes Mastodon's instance-level topology from well-known centralized social networks, which the paper states typically show positive assortativity, citing prior literature for that centralized-network baseline rather than measuring it directly in this paper.

### Stated limitations
The paper states the crawl's coverage of non-Mastodon Fediverse instances is necessarily partial, because such instances and users were discovered only incidentally through Mastodon-instance API responses, and the paper explicitly hedges its own Expanded-Instances findings on this basis: "although we detected non-Mastodon users and instances through Mastodon ones, and hence this knowledge of the Fediverse might be partial." It states it could not determine the online/offline status of instances in the earlier (Zignani et al.) comparison dataset at the time that dataset was itself created, so the paper's own claim of Mastodon reaching structural stability rests partly on a hypothesis ("we hypothesize that...Mastodon reached its stability") rather than on data available for both time points. It states explicitly that it does not analyze the content of posts or user-level social contents at all; only follow-relationship structure was collected and analyzed, and studying users' behavior and roles within and across instances is stated as the authors' planned next research stage, not something this paper addresses. The paper does not measure or report the number of user accounts hosted by each instance, or any resource-concentration metric (Gini coefficient, Nakamoto coefficient, HHI, or similar) over instance sizes; its concentration-adjacent findings (instance centrality via PageRank, core-decomposition inner-most-core sizes) measure structural position in the follow graph, not population share.

### Requirements it places on the rest of the system
Reproducing or extending this paper's Instances-network measurements requires an authenticated Mastodon API token from each instance to be crawled, since the authors state they deliberately excluded any instance that declined to grant a token rather than scraping it by other means; a study that instead scrapes public HTML would collect a different, non-directly-comparable instance population. Comparing a new crawl's structural statistics against this paper's reported values requires matching the same graph granularity (instance-to-instance aggregated follow edges, not user-to-user edges), since the paper's own Table 3 versus Table 7 versus Table 8 comparisons show density, degree, and reciprocity shift substantially between the full Instances network, the Online-Instances subgraph, and the three-years-earlier dataset purely from differences in which instances are included, independent of any real change in the platform. Any downstream claim about "Mastodon's degree of centralization" sourced to this paper must specify whether it means the instance-level follow-graph topology (what this paper measures, e.g., disassortative mixing, PageRank concentration in a persistent small set of instances) or the distribution of user accounts across instances (what this paper does not measure); conflating the two would misattribute a user-population-concentration claim to this paper's evidence.

### Contradicts
The paper's own stated motivation for retrieval in this batch (per the target registry's why_needed field) was to determine whether Raman et al.'s 2019 instance-concentration figures held, worsened, or improved as the Fediverse grew. This paper does not perform that comparison: it cites Raman, Joglekar, De Cristofaro, Sastry, Tyson, "Challenges in the Decentralised Web: The Mastodon Case" (IMC 2019) only once, as reference [7] in a list of prior Mastodon-focused studies, and never discusses or reproduces any of that paper's specific findings or figures in its body text. The paper's own point of comparison throughout is Zignani, Gaito, Rossi's 2018 ICWSM dataset and findings, not Raman et al.'s. A claim that this paper confirms, refutes, or updates Raman et al.'s concentration figures is not supported by this paper's text and must not be attributed to it.
