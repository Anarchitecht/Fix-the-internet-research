## [ARREGUI-GARCIA-ARXIV-26] On the Effects of Decentralized Moderation on Network Robustness and Information Diffusion in Mastodon
**Citation:** Beatriz Arregui-García, Lucio La Cava, Anees Baqir, Andrea Tagarelli, Riccardo Gallotti, Sandro Meloni. "On the Effects of Decentralized Moderation on Network Robustness and Information Diffusion in Mastodon." arXiv preprint, 2026.

### What it does
The paper measures whether instance-level domain blocking in Mastodon (an administrator on one instance severing all federation with another instance) produces a stable network structure over time, and whether that structure changes how information spreads across the federation. The authors build a signed, directed, temporal graph over Mastodon instances: a positive edge records that at least one user on instance i follows at least one user on instance j (a static, time-invariant layer), and a negative edge records that instance i issued a domain block against instance j on a given day (a daily, time-varying layer). They test structural stability with a discrete-time Markov model over the nine possible directed-dyad sign states, checking whether the empirical distribution of dyad states matches the stationary distribution implied by the average daily transition matrix, at increasing aggregation windows. They then partition instances into a minority group m (instances that actively issue bans) and a majority group M (instances that are predominantly banned), and simulate information diffusion on the positive network with a hybrid contagion model: simple contagion (a discrete-time SIR process, each contact with an informed neighbor informs a susceptible node with probability beta) within a group, and complex contagion (a susceptible node adopts only once the fraction of informed neighbors exceeds threshold T) across the group boundary.

### Parameters
- Observation window: August 2023 to July 2024 (one year), daily-resolution domain-block records.
- SIR simple-contagion model: infection probability beta = 1.0 used as the primary reported condition (an upper bound on within-group contagion); joint sweep of beta also performed (Figure 6/7) but the range tested is not stated as discrete numeric values in the extracted text.
- Complex-contagion threshold T: swept over [0, 1].
- Diffusion-simulation network: static network built by averaging the daily moderation (negative) layer over a one-month window (chosen because it matches the approximately 20-day equilibrium timescale identified from the KL-divergence analysis), combined with the time-invariant positive (follow) layer.
- Degree-transition analysis: in-degree and out-degree values above 100 grouped into a single category to reduce fluctuation from the long tail of the distribution.

### Stated limitations
The authors state three limitations directly. First, the analysis operates exclusively at the instance level, aggregating follower relationships and moderation actions across all users within each instance, which may obscure individual-level heterogeneity. Second, the follow network is treated as a time-invariant backbone; the authors state this is justified by the differing timescales of the moderation and following processes but that it does not capture gradual evolution of social ties over longer timescales, and that incorporating temporal follow data would give a more complete picture of how the positive and negative layers co-evolve. Third, the contagion model captures only the simple/complex distinction between within- and cross-group transmission and does not account for the semantic content of information or heterogeneous receptivity of individual instances; the authors state that a content-aware diffusion model could yield more refined predictions of which information types are most affected by moderation boundaries.

### Requirements it places on the rest of the system
The stationarity finding requires daily-resolution, longitudinal domain-block records per instance-pair over a period long enough to aggregate to multi-week windows (approximately 20 days for KL divergence to approach zero); a system that logs only current block state, without a dated history of block issuance, cannot reproduce this measurement. The information-diffusion simulation requires a positive (follow) topology and a moderation (block) topology defined over the same set of instances; the authors restrict analysis to the 5,413-instance intersection of two independently collected datasets because the two layers must be simultaneously observed on identical nodes for the signed-network construction to be well defined. The minority/majority partition (governance-active instances vs. predominantly banned instances) is derived from the empirical block-issuance data itself, so any mechanism intended to reproduce or predict this asymmetry needs the same block-issuance signal used here as ground truth.

### Contradicts
None found. This paper's minority/majority governance-asymmetry framing complements rather than contradicts ANAOBI-WWW-23's finding (same domain, different platform: Pleroma) that only a minority of instances (71.6% single-administrator, 3.5% with dedicated moderators) actively manage moderation workload.
