## [BOSHMAF-NDSS-15] Íntegro: Leveraging Victim Prediction for Robust Fake Account Detection in OSNs
**Citation:** Yazan Boshmaf, Dionysios Logothetis, Georgos Siganos, Jorge Lería, Jose Lorenzo, Matei Ripeanu, Konstantin Beznosov. "Íntegro: Leveraging Victim Prediction for Robust Fake Account Detection in OSNs." NDSS, 2015.
**Retrieved:** full text via https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss2015/integro-leveraging-victim-prediction-robust-fake-account-detection-osns
**Source URL:** https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss2015/integro-leveraging-victim-prediction-robust-fake-account-detection-osns
**Domain:** F

### What it does
Íntegro ranks accounts in an online social network (OSN) so that real accounts are placed above fake accounts in the ranked list even when fakes have deliberately friended real users who accept requests from strangers (victims), an adversary strategy that defeats prior ranking schemes relying purely on the assumption that fake accounts stay sparsely connected to real accounts. The mechanism has three stages.

First, a victim classifier trained with a random forest algorithm on low-cost, readily available profile features (friend count, photo count, feed-item count, account age, and similar attributes that require no expensive backend query) predicts, for every real account, the probability that it will accept friend requests from fakes (a vulnerability score). Second, every edge in the social graph is reweighted according to the vulnerability scores of its two endpoints: an edge incident to a predicted victim is assigned a low weight relative to edges between two accounts unlikely to be victims, using the formula w(vi, vj) that depends on the vulnerability scores p(vi) and p(vj) and two tuning constants alpha and beta. Third, trust (a numeric quantity, analogous to the trust used by prior random-walk-based rankers) is initialized on a set of manually verified trusted accounts (seeds) selected across communities detected by the Louvain method, then propagated through the weighted graph for omega = O(log n) iterations of power iteration (n is the number of nodes), and each account's degree-normalized trust after omega iterations becomes its rank; accounts are sorted by this rank in descending order.

Because edges into the fake region carry low weight whenever they pass through a predicted victim, the total amount of trust that can flow from the real region into the fake region is bounded by the sum of the weights on attack edges (edges directly joining a real and a fake account), called vol(E_a), regardless of how many attack edges exist — this is the mechanism's core robustness property, contrasted against prior schemes (SybilRank) whose bound instead grows with the raw count of attack edges.

### Stated limitations
The authors state two design limitations inherited from SybilRank and similar power-iteration ranking schemes. First, Íntegro's design is stated to apply only to undirected social graphs, because directed graphs have a substantially smaller mixing time than their undirected counterparts, making short random walks unsuitable for robust ranking on directed graphs. Second, Íntegro defers ranking newly joined accounts because their sparse initial connectivity would otherwise place them at low rank and generate false positives; the authors measured that only 7% of new users active in the prior 30 days on Tuenti had more than 46 friends (versus a platform average of 254), and that 6% of a 100-account random sample of these deferred new accounts were fake. The authors explicitly state that handling this trade-off (excluding new accounts versus risking false positives against them) is an operational decision left to the platform operator, and note a vouching-based alternative exists in other published fake-account detection designs for admitting legitimate new users, without evaluating it themselves. The authors state Íntegro is not a standalone fake-account detection system but is intended to complement existing detection mechanisms; in the Tuenti deployment it operated alongside a separate feature-based detection system and the existing user-reporting system. The community-detection step explicitly does not rely on the assumption that fake accounts form tightly-knit communities among themselves — the authors state this assumption does not always hold and that fakes can evade community-based detection by keeping sparse connectivity among themselves, and Íntegro is designed to tolerate an attacker who befriends either real or fake accounts without restriction.

### Requirements it places on the rest of the system
Íntegro requires a set of manually verified trusted accounts (seeds) distributed across the graph's detected communities; verification is performed by human analysts, and the paper states the number of seeds selectable is bounded by available manual-verification resources, with a stated minimum of one seed per detected community. It requires a community-detection step (the Louvain method, run before rate adjustment) over the full social graph to prevent inter-community sparsity from starving the trust-propagation process. It requires training data for the victim classifier consisting of real accounts already known to have accepted friend requests from fakes, plus a defined feature set of low-cost profile attributes that must be available for every account for the resulting feature vectors to be complete — the paper states 4 of the 18 Facebook features had no equivalent field in the Tuenti dataset. It requires the underlying graph to be undirected, per the stated limitation above. It requires an operational policy decision, external to the mechanism itself, on how to treat newly joined accounts during the deferral window, since the mechanism assigns them uninformatively low rank by design. Its security bound (Theorem 4.1) is proved only for an attacker establishing attack edges uniformly at random against a fast-mixing real region; the paper's own seed-targeting experiments show the bound's practical behavior changes under a resourceful attacker who instead targets accounts near or overlapping the seed set, with SybilRank slightly outperforming Íntegro at small attacker-to-seed distances (k<3).

### Contradicts
None found within this corpus. The paper explicitly disputes a property commonly assumed by community-detection-based fake-account approaches — that fake accounts necessarily form tightly-knit communities among themselves — citing evasion by sparse self-connectivity as a documented counter-strategy; this is a claim about other systems' assumptions, not about a specific paper in this corpus.

Pregueiro, "Aiding the Detection of Fake Accounts in Large Scale Social Networks" (SybilRank) [ref 13] — the direct comparison baseline throughout, using an unweighted power-iteration ranking with an O(n log n) bound growing with the raw count of attack edges rather than their total weight

- competing: GateKeeper [ref 20] — flow-based detection improving on SumUp, requires balanced-graph assumptions, O(n log n) time per trusted account ("ticket source")
- competing: B. Viswanath et al., using Mislove's greedy local-community-expansion algorithm [ref 19] to partition real and fake regions — O(n^2) time, evadable by sparse intra-fake-region connectivity
