## [ZUO-ICWSM-24] Understanding and Improving Content Moderation in Web3 Platforms
**Citation:** Wenrui Zuo, Raul J. Mondragón, Aravindh Raman, Gareth Tyson. "Understanding and Improving Content Moderation in Web3 Platforms." Proceedings of the International AAAI Conference on Web and Social Media (ICWSM), Vol. 18, 2024. DOI 10.1609/ICWSM.V18I1.31431.
**Retrieved:** full text via https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/31431/33591
**Source URL:** https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/31431
**Domain:** K

### What it does
This paper measures user-controlled moderation on memo.cash, a microblogging platform built on the Bitcoin Cash (BCH) blockchain with no instance operators, community moderators, or admins of any kind. Every user interaction (posts, follows, likes, and the moderation primitive itself, mutes) is recorded as a transaction in the blockchain's OP_RETURN field, so every action, including every mute, is permanent and publicly visible. Muting hides a target user's posts from the muting user's own timeline only; it does not unfollow the target, delete their posts, or affect any other viewer's timeline. A muting user's mute list is displayed publicly on their own profile, so the record of who muted whom is fully transparent. A muted user's only recourse is a direct message or a public post appealing the mute to the person who issued it; there is no platform-level appeal or override mechanism.

The paper does three things with this measured population. First, it trains classifiers on 406 per-user features (9 platform-activity features plus 397 content features, including 384-dimension BERT sentence-embedding averages) to predict which users get muted, and ranks feature importance. Second, it detects follower-graph communities (Louvain method) and measures whether mutes stay inside or cross community boundaries, using the Massey-Denton isolation index compared against an Erdos-Renyi random-mute baseline. Third, it builds a mute-recommendation system: given a mute-issuer/mute-receiver pair's six shared attributes (follower count, like count, and mutes-received count, for each of the two users), it predicts a 1-4 graded "willingness to mute" label and ranks candidate accounts for a personalized top-N mute list, comparing Random, Most-Popular, SVD, UserKNN, LightFM, and four CNN variants as the ranking model.

### Parameters
- Mute-prediction task: 406 features total (9 user/platform-activity features: creation days, profile-set flag, action count, followers, following, topic count, mutes issued, BCH wallet balance, transaction count; 13 text-derived features including VADER sentiment compound score, toxicity/profanity/threat/sexually-explicit scores, hate-word count via the HateBase API; 384 BERT sentence-embedding dimensions averaged per user, zeroed for users with no posts).
- Classifier hyperparameter search ranges (grid search): Logistic Regression penalty in {l1, l2}, C in {0.001-100}; SVM gamma in {1e-3, 1e-4} (RBF kernel), C in {1-200} (RBF) or {1-100} (linear); Random Forest n_estimators in {50,200,300,700}, max_features in {auto, sqrt, log2}; GBDT learning_rate in {0.05-0.2}, max_depth in {3,5,8}, subsample in {0.5-1.0}; GCN epochs in {100,200,500}, learning_rate in {0.01,0.05,0.1}, node embedding dimension in {100,200,300}.
- Community detection: Louvain method, resolution parameter = 1.0 (chosen because the authors cite prior work finding this value gives a satisfactory community count; not independently derived here).
- Mute-recommendation label scale: 4 discrete levels built from three observed behaviors (self-mute, follower-mute, like presence) — label 4 (very negative, self-mute + follower-mute + no like, 525 events), label 3 (moderately negative, self-mute only + no like, 1,825 events), label 2 (somewhat negative, self-mute + follower-mute + at least one like, 896 events), label 1 (slightly negative, self-mute + at least one like, 2,996 events).
- Recommendation top-N evaluated: N in {5, 10, 20}.
- Recommendation evaluation: 80:20 hold-out, five-fold cross-validation, metrics accuracy/precision/recall/F1, nDCG, MRR, RMSE, MAE.

### Stated limitations
The authors state the mute-recommendation system is built on a limited memo.cash-specific dataset and that generalizing the findings requires comparison against other Web3 platforms in future work. The authors state the recommendation models are trained on data covering only 50.7% of users, producing a cold-start condition that they identify as the reason LightFM (designed to handle cold start) outperforms the alternatives tested. The authors state the CNN-based recommenders underperform because CNNs require larger training sets than the roughly 6,000-event memo.cash mute dataset provides. The authors state their features and analysis are based on interaction counts (following, liking, muting) rather than post content or topic, and describe this as making the approach feasible and scalable for other user-controlled moderation systems, framed by the authors as a design choice rather than as a limitation the paper apologizes for. In the AI/ethics checklist at the end of the paper, the authors state they addressed limitations and biases as future work in the conclusion but did not separately discuss broader societal impact.

### Requirements it places on the rest of the system
A per-user moderation signal of this kind (a mute) requires each mute to be attributable to a specific issuing user and a specific target user, and requires that record to be either directly observable (as on-chain data, in memo.cash's case) or otherwise obtainable in bulk for training — the mute-prediction classifier and the recommendation system are both trained on the full historical mute record, not a sample a single user could see. The follower-community localization result (60.9% of mutes stay within-community) requires a followership graph to already exist and be observable; the isolation-index measurement is computed directly from that graph, so any design relying on community-based mute propagation needs the same graph structure available to compute community membership. The recommendation system's six pairwise features (follower count, like count, mutes-received count, for both the issuing and the receiving user) require that both users' aggregate activity counts are visible to whatever component computes recommendations — on memo.cash this is true by construction because all of it is on-chain and public, but a design with private follow/like/mute state would need an alternate channel to supply these features. The paper measures no false-positive cost or appeal-outcome data: mute appeals happen through unstructured direct messages or public posts on memo.cash, with no measured resolution rate, so a design substituting an automated recommendation for user-initiated muting has no evidence in this paper about how often a recommended mute would be later reversed or contested.

### Contradicts
None found. The paper's own finding — that platform activity count, not hateful content, is the strongest predictor of being muted — runs directly against an intuitive assumption (that toxic/hateful content is what drives moderation action), which the paper states explicitly as its central finding rather than a contradiction of another paper in this corpus.
