## [NASRULIN-BRAINS-22] MeritRank: Sybil Tolerant Reputation for Merit-Based Tokenomics
**Citation:** Bulat Nasrulin, Georgy Ishmaev, Johan Pouwelse. "MeritRank: Sybil Tolerant Reputation for Merit-Based Tokenomics." IEEE BRAINS, 2022. DOI 10.1109/BRAINS55737.2022.9908685.
**Retrieved:** full text via https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09950
**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09950
**Domain:** F

### What it does
MeritRank computes, for each node in a decentralized system, a personalized (per-viewer) reputation score for every other node from a feedback graph of recorded contributions, so that a token-based or reward-based system can allocate rewards proportional to actual contribution rather than to token stake, while bounding — not eliminating — the reputation gain an attacker can extract by creating fake identities (Sybil nodes). Every peer i maintains its own subjective view Gi = (Vi, Ei, wi) of the global feedback graph, built by gossip propagation of feedback edges; the paper does not require any peer's view to be globally consistent, only that subjective views converge as more data propagates. A reputation score Ri(Gi, j) is computed by a peer i for a target node j over i's own subjective graph, using one of three underlying propagation algorithms: Personalized PageRank (PPR, a random walk that periodically restarts at a fixed seed node), Personalized Hitting Time (PHT, a random walk that measures the expected number of steps to first reach a target node from the seed), or MaxFlow (MFW, network-flow-based trust aggregation across all paths from seed to target).

Because feedback aggregation without restriction lets an attacker's Sybil gain grow unboundedly (the paper cites Seuken and Parkes as establishing this when all peer feedback is taken into account without restriction), MeritRank instead targets Sybil tolerance: bounding the attacker's gain — the ratio between the reputation the attacker's Sybil nodes accumulate with fake (Sybil) edges present versus with them removed — by a constant c, rather than driving the ratio to 1 (full Sybil resistance, achievable only by degenerate mechanisms such as pure direct reciprocity that discard indirect feedback entirely). Three decay mechanisms lower this bound. Transitivity decay reduces a node's reputation as a function of its distance (number of hops) from the seed node in the propagation graph, directly targeting serial attacks (an attacker chains many Sybil nodes to reach the seed). Connectivity decay reduces reputation for nodes reachable through fewer than a threshold t of distinct paths (edge- or intermediary-independent connections), targeting parallel and cycle attacks (an attacker attaches multiple Sybil nodes through one or few intermediary edges, or forms cyclic Sybil structures to trap random walks). Epoch decay reduces the weight of older contributions over time, implemented either as decay applied directly to computed reputation values or as decay applied to the underlying graph edge weights.

### Measured results
All experiments used a feedback graph derived from the MakerDAO governance forum (a decentralized autonomous organization built on Ethereum), covering all recorded forum interactions (replies, likes, posts, proposal votes) from 24 June 2019 to 26 May 2022, 153 weekly epochs. Each interaction was converted to work units using the SourceCred MakerDAO project's existing weighting convention (e.g., one like on a post contributes four work units), then compressed into a feedback graph where edge weight w(i,j) is the cumulative work-unit value of feedback from user i to user j. By epoch 153 the feedback graph held 2,057 nodes and 35,853 edges; peak activity was epoch 149, adding 1,528 new edges. A single seed node outside the graph stayed connected, with equal weights, to whichever 10 nodes were most reputable at each epoch. A 20-epoch grace period preceded every attack simulation, after which one of the top-10 most reputable nodes was selected as the attacker. Two attack patterns were simulated: a single Sybil attack (attacker adds a chosen number of Sybil nodes once, then exits) and a repeated Sybil attack (attacker adds new, zero-reputation Sybil nodes every epoch for the rest of the run). Each figure reporting a varying parameter was averaged over 10 simulation runs; the paper states the standard error across runs was small enough not to be visible on the plots.

### Stated limitations
The paper states, citing Seuken and Parkes, that Sybil tolerance is not achievable at all (the attacker's gain becomes unbounded) if a reputation mechanism takes all peer feedback into account without restriction, which is the underlying reason decay mechanisms are necessary rather than optional. The paper states that full Sybil resistance (c=1) is achievable only by mechanisms that severely limit the reputation system's utility, citing direct-reciprocity schemes such as Tit-for-Tat that ignore indirect feedback and collaboration entirely — MeritRank explicitly accepts c>1 in exchange for retaining indirect feedback. The paper states epoch decay does not enhance Sybil tolerance in its tested repeated-attack scenario and can be counterproductive: an attacker who maintains at least one attack edge and adds a new Sybil node every epoch keeps cumulative gain unaffected by epoch decay, regardless of whether decay is applied to reputation values or to edge weights. All three decay mechanisms degrade informativeness (defined as how well decay preserves the original reputation ranking and values of honest, non-attacked participants) even in the complete absence of any Sybil attack; transitivity decay in particular inflates the reputation of nodes near the seed by up to 7x at maximum decay while driving reputation for more distant honest nodes toward -1 relative deviation. The paper states its results on informativeness are graph-dependent: the MakerDAO top-100 nodes are densely connected and highly active (about 60% active in at least half of all epochs), so the measured informativeness preservation may not generalize to a network whose most reputable nodes are less consistently active. The paper explicitly states no assumption is made about the allocation mechanism that consumes MeritRank's reputation scores, beyond that higher reputation is more likely to receive equal-or-larger reward, and states that a detailed treatment of allocation-mechanism properties is out of scope. Future work is stated as exploring alternative decay heuristics to better balance the tolerance/informativeness trade-off and further investigating epoch decay's counterproductive behavior.

### Requirements it places on the rest of the system
MeritRank requires a gossip protocol (external to the reputation mechanism itself) that propagates feedback-graph updates (new edges, weight changes) between peers, and each peer's reputation computation operates only over that peer's own subjective, possibly stale or incomplete view of the graph — the mechanism does not require or produce global agreement on the graph state. It requires at least one fixed seed node whose identity and trustworthiness are established outside the mechanism (the experiments fix the seed as external and connect it to the top 10 currently most-reputable nodes); the propagation algorithms (PPR, PHT, MFW) all compute scores relative to this seed, so seed selection is a precondition the paper does not itself resolve. Reputation scores are explicitly subjective per viewer, meaning no consuming component may treat a node's MeritRank score as a single global, agreed-upon value shared across all peers — an allocation mechanism built on top of it must accept per-seed, per-viewer scores as input, and the paper explicitly declines to specify or evaluate any particular allocation mechanism (it only assumes higher reputation implies equal-or-greater expected reward). Because Sybil tolerance under this design is a bounded-gain property (c >= 1), not full Sybil resistance, a consuming component cannot assume Sybil nodes receive zero reputation; the tolerance bound depends on which decay parameters are chosen, which in turn trades off directly against preserving accurate rankings for honest participants, so the choice of decay parameters is a policy decision the mechanism exposes rather than resolves.

### Contradicts
None found within this corpus. This paper's Sybil-tolerance formalism (Definition IV.4, bounding attacker's gain by a constant c >= 1 rather than requiring c = 1) is consistent with — not a dispute of — SybilLimit's and Íntegro's per-attack-edge bounding results elsewhere in this corpus; MeritRank bounds a reputation-inflation ratio under a feedback-aggregation model, while SybilLimit and Íntegro bound a count of accepted or highly ranked Sybil identities under a social-graph acceptance model, and the paper does not claim equivalence between these formalisms.
