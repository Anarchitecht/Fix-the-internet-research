## [XAVIER-ARXIV-24] An evidence-based and critical analysis of the Fediverse decentralization promises

**Citation:** Henrique S. Xavier. "An evidence-based and critical analysis of the Fediverse decentralization promises." WebMedia 2024 (arXiv preprint), 2024. arXiv:2408.15383.
**Retrieved:** full text via https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15383
**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15383
**Domain:** J

### What it does
The paper argues, from a mix of protocol analysis and traffic measurement, that the Fediverse (the network of independently operated social media servers, called instances, that interoperate through the ActivityPub protocol) does not by itself prevent concentration of users onto a small number of instances or capture by for-profit companies. It examines what ActivityPub, the protocol governing inter-instance communication, technically enforces versus what it merely permits: a post made on one instance only propagates to a second instance if the second instance has not blocked the first and at least one user on the second follows the author, an account is bound to a single home instance and cannot post through another instance's identity, and nothing in the protocol prevents an instance from running proprietary code, injecting advertisements or algorithmic recommendations, or being operated by a for-profit or Big Tech entity. The paper then measures the actual current distribution of Fediverse users across instances using a public crawler dataset, compares monthly visit traffic between prominent Fediverse-connected domains, and draws a structural analogy to two older, similarly permissionless decentralized protocols — email (SMTP) and the World Wide Web (HTTP) — arguing from those systems' measured traffic concentration that a protocol allowing anyone to run a server does not, on its own, prevent traffic and user concentration in practice.

### Parameters
No experimental parameters in the sense of a simulation or system implementation; this is a measurement-and-argument paper drawing on two external third-party datasets: fediverse.observer (crawled Fediverse instance/user counts, snapshot date 2024-08-02) and SimilarWeb (commercial web-traffic estimation service, reporting periods March-May 2024 for the instance-traffic comparison and August-October 2023 for the email- and web-industry concentration figures).

### Stated limitations
The paper states its own future work explicitly: to survey Fediverse instances directly rather than relying on third-party crawler data (fediverse.observer), to examine ActivityPub's and the AT Protocol's selective implementation and vulnerability to manipulation in more depth, and to connect the Fediverse's anticipated evolution to broader social harms (hate speech, disinformation, mental health effects, behavioral manipulation) which this paper raises as open questions rather than measures. The paper itself notes a methodological caveat on its own webmail concentration figure: SimilarWeb excludes mail.google.com from its "Email" industry ranking because it treats it as a subdomain, so the reported industry-share concentration for Google understates the true figure, evidenced by mail.google.com's own visit count (10.88 billion) exceeding the reported industry leader live.com's count (2.19 billion). The paper notes unique-visitor counts (used for the SimilarWeb non-Fediverse comparison) are not the same measure as monthly active users (used for the fediverse.observer Fediverse figures), since active users require an authenticated login while unique visitors do not, and states the two are "close enough" to support a directional comparison without claiming numeric equivalence. Threads (Meta's ActivityPub-connected platform) is described as only one-way connected to the Fediverse: Fediverse users can follow Threads accounts, but Threads users cannot follow or view content from other Fediverse instances, so a plain sum of "Fediverse users" that includes Threads' 150 million active users overstates the interoperable population; the paper does not itself compute an adjusted combined total accounting for this asymmetry.

### Requirements it places on the rest of the system
None — this is an argument-and-measurement paper about an existing deployed protocol's observed concentration, not a description of a mechanism with implementable preconditions for another system to satisfy. Its measured concentration figures (82% of instances under 5 active users; 50% of users on the top 20 of 21,146 instances) are directly relevant as a base rate against which any newly designed federated or peer-to-peer social system's own concentration should be compared, since they establish that permitting anyone to run an independent server does not, without an additional enforced mechanism, prevent the emergence of a small number of dominant nodes.

### Contradicts
The paper explicitly states that a specific popular claim about the Fediverse — that a user can post using an account from any instance the way one might expect from a fully peer-to-peer system — is inaccurate, and clarifies that posting requires an account specific to the instance being posted from, with cross-instance visibility gated by both the receiving instance's block list and its users' follow relationships. This is presented as a correction of a claim commonly made about the Fediverse's flexibility, not a disagreement with another paper in this corpus.
