## [BARTUSEK-EPRINT-26] Fair-Weather No More: Guaranteed Efficiency in Secure Group Messaging

**Citation:** James Bartusek, Nir Bitansky, Yevgeniy Dodis, Rachit Garg, David J. Wu. "Fair-Weather No More: Guaranteed Efficiency in Secure Group Messaging." IACR Cryptology ePrint Archive, 2026. DOI unassigned.
**Retrieved:** full text via https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1677.pdf
**Source URL:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1677.pdf
**Domain:** H

### What it does
The paper builds the first continuous group key agreement (CGKA — a primitive that maintains one shared secret among a changing group of users, where any member, not a central manager, may add or remove members or refresh keys) with a computation and communication cost per operation that is provably polylogarithmic in the group size in the worst case, not merely in the best case.

The construction routes CGKA through a new primitive the paper calls incremental distributed broadcast encryption (DBE — a scheme where each user samples an independent public/private keypair with no coordination, and any sender can encrypt one ciphertext that every user in a chosen set can decrypt with its own private key plus the other members' public keys). Incremental means a user's aggregated public key and each member's aggregated secret key can be updated to reflect one added or removed member without recomputing from the full membership. A CGKA scheme built directly on incremental DBE broadcasts a fresh group secret, encrypted under the current aggregated public key, on every Add, Remove, or refresh operation; this variant achieves post-compromise security (PCS — the group recovers a secure state through normal operations after an honest member's secret state was exposed) with every operation staying polylogarithmic, because the incremental update and the encryption both avoid touching all n members' keys.

Forward secrecy (FS — past group keys stay secret even after a current member's state is later exposed) needs a second primitive, updatable DBE, built in the random oracle model (a proof technique that treats a specified hash function as an ideal random function) on the same lattice assumption. Updatable DBE lets a sender implicitly rotate every receiver's secret key while sending one compact ciphertext, so a member who never sends an update is still protected against future compromise revealing past secrets. Add and Remove stay polylogarithmic in the updatable-DBE-based scheme; the one operation the paper does not make sublinear is a dedicated forward-secrecy-refresh operation, which is allowed to take time linear in group size and is the only such operation in the scheme.

### Measured results
None. This is a cryptographic construction and security-proof paper; every stated bound is asymptotic (a function of group size n and security parameter λ) under a stated hardness assumption, not a measured latency, bandwidth, or throughput figure from any implementation or deployment. The paper states its own lattice parameters are likely too large for a practical deployment without further optimization not attempted in this work.

### Stated limitations
The paper states the PCS-only construction achieves only passive security: the adversary may read honest members' secret states over time but cannot craft messages on their behalf or control message delivery order beyond dropping or delaying messages, matching the security model of Bienstock et al. (BIENSTOCK-TCC-22). It states security against active adversaries who forge or reorder messages is left to future work, though the authors argue informally that their scheme should resist drop and delay attacks. It states the lattice parameters needed for the current proof are likely too large for real-world use without further, unattempted optimization. It states, as its own explicit open problem, that no construction is known that achieves worst-case sublinear complexity for forward secrecy in computation, not merely communication — the FS-refresh operation stays linear-time in every construction in the paper. It reports, in an appendix, a concrete attack that breaks an aggressive sublinear-time variant of the updatable DBE scheme, offered as partial evidence that the linear-time FS-refresh is not easily removed by the paper's own technique. It states the paper's improvement is over the framework of Bienstock et al. (BIENSTOCK-TCC-22) and does not claim to remove that paper's black-box impossibility result — that result rules out sublinear worst-case CGKA built from public-key encryption used as a sealed component (its internal algebraic structure not accessed), and this paper's construction avoids that barrier by using the internal lattice structure of its encryption scheme directly, not by refuting the impossibility result.

### Requirements it places on the rest of the system
The construction requires a common reference string (crs) sampled once at setup, shared by all group members. It requires each party to store the full current group roster and every other current member's public key, so any component using this CGKA must supply and keep that per-member public-key list current at every party. It requires message delivery to be honest, in the sense that the adversary cannot forge or actively tamper with the content of add, remove, or refresh messages — active-adversary security is explicitly not proved. Achieving forward secrecy requires periodically running the dedicated FS-refresh operation; because that operation costs communication and computation linear in group size, a system that wants both efficiency and up-to-date forward secrecy must decide how often to pay that linear cost, and the paper states the model is meaningful specifically where network bandwidth is far more expensive than local per-party computation, or where members run FS-refresh infrequently and accept a correspondingly weaker forward-secrecy guarantee between refreshes.

### Contradicts
None found against another corpus entry's measurement — this paper reports no measurements. It directly narrows, but explicitly does not refute, the impossibility scope of BIENSTOCK-TCC-22: that paper shows no CGKA built black-box from public-key encryption avoids Ω(n) worst-case communication, and this paper's own construction sidesteps that scope by using a non-black-box, structured lattice-based primitive (incremental and updatable distributed broadcast encryption) rather than generic public-key encryption. The paper states TreeKEM (the CGKA underlying RFC 9420/BARNES-RFC-23) does not achieve forward secrecy in general, citing Alwen, Coretti, Dodis, Tselekounis (CRYPTO 2020) as having demonstrated this, and reports that a cited independent experimental paper (Auerbach, Cueto Noval, Erol, Pietrzak, CRYPTO 2025) measured TreeKEM's communication complexity converging toward linear in a random-update scenario, reaching Ω(√n) at update-batch size d=1 and Ω(n^0.99) at d=50 — a measurement the present paper reports as a citation, not one it produced itself, so it is not usable in this evidence file as this paper's own measured result.
