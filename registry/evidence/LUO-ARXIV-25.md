## [LUO-ARXIV-25] Unveiling Ethereum's P2P Network: The Role of Chain and Client Diversity

**Citation:** Jiahao Luo. "Unveiling Ethereum's P2P Network: The Role of Chain and Client Diversity." arXiv preprint, 2025. DOI 10.48550/ARXIV.2501.16236.
**Retrieved:** full text via https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16236
**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16236
**Domain:** J

### What it does
The paper measures how many peers discoverable in Ethereum's peer-to-peer overlay (devp2p, the protocol stack combining a node-discovery layer, the RLPx encrypted-transport layer, and application sub-protocols) are actually reachable and configuration-compatible with a mainnet full node, and separates that measurement from the underlying causes: node-discovery protocol non-compliance and multi-chain (fork and sidechain) fragmentation of the shared discovery table. The authors deploy five instrumented Geth (Go Ethereum, the reference full-node client) instances that log every discovery-protocol message (Ping, Pong, FindNode, Neighbors) and RLPx handshake (Hello, Status) they send or receive, store the decoded messages in a database, and classify every discovered peer into one of three categories: no sub-protocol exchange attempted, sub-protocol exchange attempted but incompatible, or fully compatible. The tool additionally tracks each peer's self-reported client software identifier and version string from the RLPx Hello message, which lets the authors attribute specific protocol deviations (message counts, disconnect reasons, IP-address bugs) to specific client implementations and versions rather than to the network as a whole.

### Parameters
- Node-discovery bucket size increased from the devp2p-specified 16 entries to 1,048,576 to widen the local routing table.
- Neighbors-message size capped at the specification's 16-node maximum to avoid amplification.
- Concurrent node-discovery tasks increased from Geth's default of 3 to 1,000, reducing time-to-connect-to-5,000-nodes from ~4 days to ~10 hours in the authors' own configuration.
- Dial-scheduler source count increased from 1 to 50, giving ~100 dials/second/node.
- Connection attempts throttled to a maximum of 10 per peer per minute network-wide.
- DHT (distributed hash table) refresh interval reduced from the default 5 minutes to 10 seconds.
- Geth's own default FindNode/Neighbors timeout: 1.5 seconds when fewer than 16 nodes are received (a Geth-internal parameter the paper measures the consequences of, not one the authors set).
- Measurement infrastructure: 5 Geth v1.14.0 instances, Ubuntu 22.04, 1 Gb/s bandwidth, 256 GB RAM, 64 cores per instance; MongoDB storage capped at 64 GB memory use; bandwidth usage self-limited to 1 Gbps and outbound transaction-request replies limited to half of received requests, stated as ethical/network-impact bounds rather than tuned experimental variables.

### Stated limitations
The paper does not report an aggregate, network-wide client-implementation market-share figure (e.g., "Geth holds X% of all Ethereum nodes"); every client-attribution figure in this paper is conditional on a specific narrow subpopulation — the 156 reachable peers with the private-IP bug, the 1,861 Nethermind peers examined for breach disconnects, or the per-client Neighbors-response-length table — and none of these subpopulations is presented as representative of the full peer population's client composition. Readers requiring an overall client-share concentration figure for Ethereum are not served by this paper; that number is not present in the retrieved text. The authors state "the full extent of these issues on overall network stability remains unclear" regarding the private-IP and peer-scoring bugs they identify. The paper's own comparison figures against Kim et al. (2018) are stated as taken from that prior study, not independently re-derived by this paper's own methodology on the earlier dataset, so the two measurements are not a controlled before/after comparison under identical instrumentation. The paper does not evaluate mobile or light-client peers, nor does it measure bandwidth or latency costs of the diversity-driven inefficiencies it identifies (it measures connection-success rates and disconnect/timeout counts, not throughput or user-facing latency).

### Requirements it places on the rest of the system
A shared node-discovery table (a single DHT serving Ethereum mainnet, Ethereum testnets, and unrelated EVM-compatible chains such as Polygon and BNB Smart Chain simultaneously, as measured here) requires an expensive per-candidate handshake (TCP connection, encryption, protocol Hello/Status exchange) before a peer's chain membership can be determined, so any design reusing a shared discovery layer across multiple independent chains or forks inherits a connection-success rate that falls as the number of incompatible chains sharing that layer grows — measured here at 2.47% fully compatible after 500 million dial attempts, down from 13.07% at 1 million attempts. A discovery protocol whose per-message size is capped by the underlying transport's datagram limit (UDP's 1,280-byte practical limit here, constraining Neighbors responses to 12-16 entries depending on IPv4 versus IPv6 addressing) requires every participating client implementation to agree on the exact node count per response, because this paper measures that implementations disagreeing on that count each other's messages does not error cleanly but instead produces timeouts (Geth waiting its full 1.5-second timeout for the fewer-than-16-node responses that Besu, Nethermind, and Bor send). A liveness/heartbeat message field used to populate address or scoring data (the discv4 Ping message's fromIP field feeding Geth's DHT bucket-ordering and Besu/Nethermind's peer-scoring) requires validation against the transport-layer source address, because this paper measures multiple client implementations (Geth via its UPnP-NAT fallback, CoreGeth, and its ETCMC fork) propagating unverified private/loopback addresses from that field into peer records.

### Contradicts
The paper's title and framing invite the expectation of an overall Ethereum client-market-share concentration figure, but no such aggregate figure appears in the retrieved text — a claim of "Ethereum node client concentration measured by Luo 2025" citing a specific percentage split across Geth/Nethermind/Besu/Erigon for the whole network would not be supported by this paper's own reported tables and must not be carried forward from this entry. No other paper in this batch measures Ethereum P2P network structure, so no cross-paper numeric contradiction exists in this batch.
