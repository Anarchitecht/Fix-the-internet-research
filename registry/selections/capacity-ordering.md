## Capacity-heterogeneous participation: A capacity-selected minority carries all relay traffic while everyone else is excluded from relay duty by structural role, and no mechanism in this corpus defends the capacity claim that selection runs on

### Selection

The selected mechanism excludes low-capacity peers from relay duty by structural role rather than by throttling their share of it: a minority of peers, selected by claimed capacity, forms a transit-carrying tier that every other peer connects to but never routes through — the two-tier design measured in [YANG-ICDE-03] and corroborated at real-network scale in [LOO-IPTPS-04]. Within that transit-carrying tier, routing is recommended to be ordered by capacity so that no transit-tier member ever sits on a path between two higher-capacity members, which [YANG-ICDE-03] states directly is an orthogonal, pluggable choice at that layer — "the use of super-peers and the choice of routing protocol are orthogonal issues." The specific mechanism recommended for that layer is the bandwidth-ordered self-stabilizing overlay commonly attributed to Feldotto, Scheideler, and Graffi (HSkip+, P2P 2014). **This half of the selection is a labeled pick, not a corpus-backed selection**: no full-text entry for that paper exists anywhere in this corpus's evidence file, so its specific bounds cannot be independently checked here. What can be checked is that a systematic 2021 survey of the entire self-stabilizing-overlay literature confirms the design exists and remains, as of that survey's own forward-mining check, the only design in its coverage window that orders routing by node capacity at all [FELDMANN-CSUR-21]. Anyone building this needs to retrieve Feldotto, Scheideler, and Graffi directly and re-verify the specific bounds before relying on them.

**Against uniform assignment with per-participant budgets:** [SAROIU-MMCN-02] measures bandwidth, latency, availability, and shared-content volume varying "between three and five orders of magnitude" across a combined 1.75-million-peer Napster and Gnutella crawl, and states directly, as a requirement any uniform-treatment system inherits, that such a system "requires a mechanism to detect and differentially weight peer capability rather than treating peers as interchangeable." [BLAKE-HOTOS-03] derives, from an independent 33,000-host Gnutella crawl, that storing 1 TB of unique data at high availability is effectively infeasible under Gnutella-like uniform participation and cable-modem-like bandwidth, and separately measures that restricting membership to the top 5% of hosts by availability cuts required maintenance bandwidth roughly 1,000-fold relative to the least favorable uniform-participation point. [YANG-ICDE-03]'s own measured comparison is the direct rebuttal: moving from cluster size 1 (uniform) to a capacity-selected cluster size of 10 at matched query reach reduces bandwidth and processing load over 79%. Uniform assignment is rejected on this evidence, not merely disfavored.

**Against local proportional-response allocation (PropShare) as a standalone answer:** [LEVIN-SIGCOMM-08] proves PropShare's allocation rule Sybil-proof against splitting a fixed contribution across identities (§6.3), the strongest defense of this specific kind anywhere in this corpus — but the rule only decides how much bandwidth flows on a link two already-connected, already-interested peers share. It answers nothing about whether a low-capacity peer sits on a path between two other peers in the first place, which is the property this component needs. Its own family demonstrates the gap directly: a client requesting from far more peers than the reciprocity rule assumes, and uploading nothing, still completed every one of 7 real BitTorrent downloads [LOCHER-HOTNETS-06], and a large-view free-rider outperformed compliant peers below a 40% free-rider population share [SIRIVIANOS-IPTPS-07] — both exploit the unconditional admission path every allocation-only mechanism in this family still needs for bootstrapping, not the proportional-split formula itself. PropShare is retained, not rejected outright, as the allocation rule for bandwidth already flowing between peers once the topology-level selection above has already excluded low-capacity peers from transit duty — see the requirements below.

**Against redirecting flow toward high-capacity peers without a formal tier (Lv, Ratnasamy, Shenker):** no evidence entry for this paper exists in this corpus. It is recorded here, per this project's own rule that a candidate with no evidence entry is listed and marked as having none, rather than silently omitted. No comparison against the selection can be made on evidence; a future pass should retrieve it directly, since [YANG-ICDE-03]'s own bibliography note suggests it addresses the identical problem this component exists to solve, without introducing the single-point-of-failure-per-client property the two-tier design carries.

**Against adopting one of the misreport-defense mechanisms (PeerFlow, FlashFlow, Proof of Backhaul, TorCoin) as the primary selection, rather than the two-tier structure plus capacity-ordered routing:** each of these four answers a different question — how much to trust a claimed number — not the question this component is about, which is who is asked to relay at all and in what order. [JOHNSON-POPETS-17] states PeerFlow "requires a functioning Directory Authority infrastructure"; [TRAUDT-ICDCS-21] states FlashFlow "requires the same Directory Authority (DirAuth) and Bandwidth Authority (BW Auth) infrastructure Tor already runs"; [SHENG-NDSS-24] requires a coordinator able to draw "a fresh, randomly drawn subset of challengers... from a larger active pool" whose own freedom from Sybil control the mechanism does not itself supply; [GHOSH-HOTPETS-14] requires an already-functioning, majority-honest assignment-server quorum and, even then, produces only a per-circuit proof at mint time, not a general per-node capacity value a topology or ordering rule could sort by. None of the four supplies a role-assignment or routing-order mechanism; each is a verification layer that could feed a truer capacity number into the selected structure, at the mechanical cost detailed below, not a replacement for it.

### What this selection requires from the rest of the system

- A bootstrap/discovery mechanism that assigns a joining node to a transit-tier position or to a specific transit-tier neighbor set, without funneling every join through one centralized rendezvous point — [YANG-ICDE-03] states this is unsolved by its own design ("an open problem in pure and super-peer networks, with many potential solutions") and no other entry in this corpus closes it.
- A capacity value to sort or select by, at minimum a self-declared one; and — because nothing in this selection defends that value against a false claim — either a Sybil-resistance mechanism from the identity component keeping any single coalition's claimed aggregate capacity under whatever coalition-weight threshold a verification layer requires (PeerFlow's bound holds only "even without any trusted relays" if a coalition stays under trim threshold λ=0.256 [JOHNSON-POPETS-17]), or an explicit acceptance that admission to the transit tier and rank within it can be gamed, recorded as an open risk rather than assumed away.
- Continuous per-client metadata synchronization to the assigned transit-tier node at join, update, and leave — the transit node's ability to answer on a client's behalf is only as current as this channel [YANG-ICDE-03].
- If k-redundancy is adopted to reduce the single-point-of-failure property of a client's one assigned transit-tier connection, every neighboring transit-tier node must open k connections instead of one, an O(k²) growth in transit-tier connection count the underlying transport/NAT-traversal component must provision for [YANG-ICDE-03].
- If the bandwidth-ordered self-stabilizing routing layer is adopted among transit-tier members, it requires an external failure detector or identifier-assignment scheme guaranteeing node-identifier correctness before the protocol begins, an oracle for permanent-departure detection (no purely local rule can supply one — [FELDMANN-CSUR-21], Theorem 7.2), and a transport exposing full neighbor-state inspection on demand [JACOB-JACM-14].
- Once transit-tier admission has already excluded low-capacity peers, the local proportional-response rule (PropShare) is required as the within-tier allocation mechanism deciding how much bandwidth flows on each already-established transit-tier link, consuming an observable per-round received-bandwidth signal per neighbor [LEVIN-SIGCOMM-08] and a swarm/interest membership source supplying the candidate peer set [LEVIN-SIGCOMM-08].
- If a PeerFlow-style peer-measurement layer is added to bound self-report inflation, it requires per-peer, per-position application-layer byte accounting at each measuring node, a small aggregation quorum running trim/probation/increase-cap logic, and differential-privacy noise injection before publishing aggregated statistics, since raw peer-to-peer byte counts otherwise leak which pairs exchanged how much traffic [JOHNSON-POPETS-17].
- If a FlashFlow-style active load-test layer is added instead, it requires a dedicated measurer-bandwidth allowance exceeding the highest-capacity target by a factor of roughly 2.84 at recommended parameters, provisioned on an ongoing basis separately from relay bandwidth, plus synchronized clocks and a shared secure-randomness source across whatever quorum runs the measurement schedule [TRAUDT-ICDCS-21].

### What it costs and where it fails

At the redesign scale [YANG-ICDE-03] models (a 20,000-node network, cluster size 10, matched to a 3,000-node query reach), the two-tier structure reduces incoming bandwidth, outgoing bandwidth, and processing power by over 79% against uniform assignment, and cuts individual load for the lowest 90% of nodes by one to two orders of magnitude. Cluster size carries an unresolved numerical tension the source paper states directly rather than closes: individual load favors small clusters, aggregate load and per-cluster efficiency favor large ones, and reliability (fewer single points of failure per client) favors small ones, with the paper stating only that "the answer to this question is specific to the application and situation" [YANG-ICDE-03]. A single transit-tier node's failure disconnects every one of its clients until a replacement is located; k=2 redundancy lowers a partner's individual load about 48% at cluster size 100 at the cost of roughly 2.5% higher aggregate bandwidth and 17% higher aggregate processing load, and does not eliminate the disconnection window, only shorten it [YANG-ICDE-03]. A single transit-tier node that unilaterally raises its own capacity (equivalently, its own declared or acted-on outdegree) sees its own load rise 303%, while the identical change adopted network-wide instead lowers that node's load 14% [YANG-ICDE-03] — a first-mover penalty against acting on a truthfully higher capacity that exists independent of any adversary, and one that points in the same direction as a measured real-world finding: peers in a large deployed system under-report their own capacity in a direction consistent with avoiding attracting requests, with up to 30% of self-declared-low-bandwidth users measured to hold significantly higher true bandwidth [SAROIU-MMCN-02]. Real-deployment churn data show the transit tier is only moderately more stable than the leaf population it protects, not dramatically so: measured average connection lifetime was 93 minutes for ultrapeers against 58 minutes for leaves, a 1.5x difference, not an order of magnitude [LOO-IPTPS-04].

The recommended bandwidth-ordered routing layer among transit-tier members fails under concurrent churn during convergence, per the general statement in [FELDMANN-CSUR-21] that termination "may not be reached if the node set changes during convergence" and that no bound in this literature covers concurrent join/leave rate — a statement in evident tension with this project's own unsourced claim that HSkip+ specifically survives 60% random and 35% adversarial churn, a tension this corpus supplies no way to resolve (see below). Neither half of this selection defends against a misreported capacity value at the point that value determines admission to the transit tier or rank within it: [YANG-ICDE-03] states its own "limited altruism" assumption explicitly does not address freeloading or malicious refusal, and no entry attributed to HSkip+ in this corpus addresses misreport at all. Layering in a verification mechanism narrows, but does not close, that gap, at a measured price: PeerFlow's aggregation quorum adds 119.6 bytes/second average per-authority reporting overhead and up to 2.38 KiB/s network-wide dummy-traffic overhead under its highest-trim setting, and takes a median 27.7 hours (75th percentile 70.7 hours, worst case 316.4 hours) to establish a given relay's measured capacity [JOHNSON-POPETS-17]; FlashFlow's dedicated measurement team requires standing bandwidth provisioned at roughly 2.84x the target's own claimed capacity, an ongoing operational allowance rather than a one-time charge, and both mechanisms depend on a small semi-trusted quorum this design's own goal of resisting single-organization capture argues against reintroducing at scale [TRAUDT-ICDCS-21].

### What the corpus does not settle

- HSkip+ itself (Feldotto, Scheideler, Graffi, P2P 2014) has no full-text entry under `registry/evidence` in this corpus. Every specific figure attributed to it in this document — the O(log²n)/O(log⁴n) message-count comparison, the bandwidth-ordering routing property, the 60%/35% churn-survival claim, the O(log n) dilation and congestion bounds — is sourced only to this project's own already-verified reading list, not to an independently retrievable primary source this synthesis, or a future one, can check. Retrieving the paper directly and entering it as a full evidence-file record is a precondition for treating any of those figures as settled.
- The apparent tension between [FELDMANN-CSUR-21]'s general statement that this literature's self-stabilization convergence proofs assume a static node set during convergence, and the specific claim that HSkip+ survives 60% random and 35% adversarial churn, is not adjudicated by anything in this corpus. The survey does not cite a churn bound for HSkip+ itself; whether HSkip+'s own paper proves a churn-tolerance result outside the base model the survey describes is unknown here.
- No entry in this corpus measures or proves a defense against a false capacity claim made specifically to gain a structural position — admission to a transit tier, or rank within a capacity-ordered routing structure — in an open-membership overlay with no privileged administrative quorum. `registry/open-problems/honest-capacity-reporting.md` records this as its own verdict of "open" after an independent search, and states directly that the nearest published defenses (PeerFlow, FlashFlow) bound a *selection probability* over a flat, unordered relay set, a different quantity from the *position* a capacity-ordered structure assigns, and that neither was designed, tested, or discussed by its own authors as an ordering input. That same open-problem entry also reports two papers checked in full but not entered as their own evidence-file records in this corpus — `PATEL-ARXIV-25` (a September 2025 survey of secure peer-to-peer networks) and `IHLE-CSUR-23` (a 2023 systematic review of peer-to-peer incentive mechanisms) — so their findings are relayed here at one remove, through that open-problem document, rather than independently verified by this selection.
- No entry composes the local proportional-response rule (PropShare) with a two-tier or bandwidth-ordered admission layer running above it. [LEVIN-SIGCOMM-08] evaluates PropShare only within an already-formed BitTorrent swarm of directly interested peers; how it behaves layered under a supernode cluster or a capacity-ordered routing structure is untested anywhere in this corpus.
- No entry quantifies what fraction of a population matching [SAROIU-MMCN-02]'s measured heterogeneity, filtered specifically by cellular or metered-connection status rather than merely low peak bandwidth, would fall below any candidate's actual admission threshold for the transit tier. [DOAN-NETWORKING-20] measures a mobile client's own retrieval performance over cellular versus WiFi (startup delay, path length, round-trip time to a content gateway), not a mobile device's suitability or exclusion as a transit node for other peers' traffic, so it does not supply this number either.
- Bootstrapping — how a joining node is assigned to the transit tier or to a specific transit-tier neighbor set without a centralized directory — is stated as an open problem by [YANG-ICDE-03] itself and is not closed by any other entry in this corpus.
- Lv, Ratnasamy, and Shenker's alternative ("Can Heterogeneity Make Gnutella Scalable?", IPTPS 2002), which [YANG-ICDE-03]'s own bibliography describes as redirecting flow toward high-capacity nodes without a formal supernode tier, has no full-text entry anywhere in this corpus and cannot be compared against the selection on evidence.
