## Repair: local-group exact reconstruction cuts repair traffic to a third or a half of plain Reed-Solomon's, but no source in the corpus tests any coded repair mechanism under both open-participant churn and an adversarial helper

### Candidates

### Selection

Locally Repairable Codes, in the Azure/Xorbas construction — local-group exact reconstruction as the default repair path, with a full Reed-Solomon decode retained as the fallback when a group loses more members than its local parity covers.

Against full reconstruction from k of n: LRC is preferred because it is the only coded candidate with two independent real production deployments confirming the same directional result — Microsoft Azure Storage (`HUANG-ATC-12`) and Facebook's Hadoop cluster (`SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13`) — each showing a 2x-or-better cut in fragments read per repair at matched or lower storage overhead than plain Reed-Solomon. Plain reconstruction is not rejected on security grounds; both mechanisms share the same helper-returns-its-own-stored-bytes property, so both get the same free content-hash verification. It is rejected on the traffic and reachability axes the corpus measures directly: `HUANG-ATC-12`'s own (6,2,2) example shows RS(6,3) reading all 6 fragments where LRC(6,2,2) reads 3 for the identical repair, its production (12,2,2) deployment reads 6 fragments where the matched-overhead RS(12,4) comparison reads 12, and Storj's deployed RS configuration needs k=29 holders reachable at once (`LI-IWQOS-23-STORJ`) against LRC's group sizes of 3-6 in the same table.

Against the full-replica hybrid: the hybrid's single-holder repair path is cheaper in the common case than LRC's local group, but `DIMAKIS-TIT-10` measures a regenerating code (a different coded mechanism, cited here only for the comparison point against Hybrid) beating Hybrid by up to 58x less bandwidth at 100x lower unavailability on the PlanetLab trace, and `RODRIGUES-IPTPS-05` — the hybrid's own source paper — states its own resulting bandwidth (~100 kbps/node on the Overnet trace) as unsustainable for home participants. A design placing a full, unfragmented copy of every object on one designated node also reintroduces the single-point-of-concentration problem erasure coding exists to avoid, at a scale (one full object per node) that a storage-constrained participant device cannot always accommodate; no source in the corpus measures this constraint, so it is stated here as a structural consequence of the mechanism's own description, not a further citation.

Against exact-regenerating codes at MSR/MBR: `VAJHA-FAST-18` is the corpus's only real-deployment measurement of this family, and it shows the mechanism's headline traffic reduction (2.9x over RS) realized at d=19 simultaneously reachable helpers, out of 19 surviving nodes in a 20-node stripe — a larger simultaneous-reachability requirement than LRC's 3-6, on the exact axis the brief calls out for this component. `DIMAKIS-TIT-10`'s own Gnutella-trace result shows the family's bandwidth advantage over a plain hybrid nearly vanishing on the corpus's least-stable real trace, which is the trace closest in character to an open volunteer population (`ANDERSON-CCGRID-06`'s BOINC host lifetime, 89.5 days average, sits closer to Gnutella's turnover than to PlanetLab's 2.23-year mean disk lifetime). A second, independent reason favors LRC specifically for this architecture: MSR/MBR repair has each helper transmit a linear combination it computes fresh for that one repair event, not bytes it already stored and hashed, so no existing content-addressing check can verify a helper's response — `RASHMI-TIT-11`, `VAJHA-FAST-18`, and `PATRA-TIT-25` supply no commitment scheme to close that gap, and `PATRA-TIT-25`'s own adversary model stops short of a helper that can falsify its computation, not only its stored data.

Against two-dimensional erasure coding with vector commitments (RedStuff): RedStuff is the only candidate in the corpus that deliberately closes the same on-the-fly-verification gap that exact-regenerating codes leave open, and it is the only candidate measured on a real permissionless-committee deployment moving real repair traffic (`DANEZIS-WALRUS-25`'s epoch 17/20 figures). It is not selected as the default because its measured deployment is not the population this component's economics question is about: Walrus's committee nodes are staked and report median 55 TB capacity, ≥16 CPU cores, 128 GB RAM, and 1 Gbps bandwidth — provisioned infrastructure, not the volunteer or consumer-hardware population `BLAKE-HOTOS-03` and `ANDERSON-CCGRID-06` measure. Its 4.5x storage overhead is also the highest of any candidate here, against LRC's 1.33-1.6x, an overhead `DANEZIS-WALRUS-25` itself states goes specifically toward Byzantine tolerance and reduced recovery bandwidth together, not toward repair traffic alone. Where the architecture needs verified repair against a genuinely adversarial helper population — which an open participant network, unlike Azure's or Facebook's own datacenters, cannot rule out — RedStuff's construction is the corpus's only evidence that the problem is solvable at all; it is treated below, in the open-problem discussion, as the fallback for that case, rather than folded into the primary selection.

### What this selection requires from the rest of the system

- The object encoder must place k data blocks into l local groups of fixed, system-known membership (group size r, plus one local parity per group), and every node performing placement or repair must be able to look up a given block's group members — `PAPAILIOPOULOS-TIT-14`, `SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13`.
- The object's manifest must carry a per-fragment content hash, established at encode time and itself authenticated (signed by the publisher, or covered by the parent object's own root hash), so a repairing node can verify each local-group member's returned bytes before combining them. This is the mechanism that gives LRC repair Byzantine tolerance without RedStuff's vector-commitment machinery; no retrieved paper implements or measures it, so it is stated here as a requirement this selection needs supplied, not as an evidenced property.
- The system must implement full Reed-Solomon decode as a fallback ("heavy") path for the case a local group loses more members than its local parity covers — `SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13`'s light/heavy decoder split — meaning this selection does not eliminate row 1's mechanism, it only reduces how often it is invoked.
- Local-group coefficients must be chosen so every linear system a repair could need to solve stays invertible over the working finite field; `SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13` defers this construction to an appendix rather than a closed-form rule a builder can apply directly (see "What the corpus does not settle").
- Each group's members must be placed in distinct fault domains, or the equivalent for a peer population (distinct autonomous systems, distinct operators) — `HUANG-ATC-12`'s reliability figures assume this and do not hold without it.
- A liveness-tracking / repair-triggering layer must supply a membership timeout distinguishing transient disconnection from permanent departure: `BLAKE-HOTOS-03` measures up to a 30x maintenance-bandwidth swing from this one parameter alone, independent of which fragment-repair mechanism is chosen. `CHUN-NSDI-06`'s reintegration policy (Carbonite) is the corpus's strongest measured result for this layer — 44% overhead above an oracle that could distinguish permanent from transient failure perfectly, against roughly 2x oracle overhead for a threshold policy that discards and re-replicates on every disconnection (`BHAGWAN-NSDI-04`'s eager repair, and the "Cates" comparison point in `CHUN-NSDI-06`) — and needs no measurement of host availability `a` to achieve it, unlike `BHAGWAN-NSDI-04`'s lazy-repair policy, which requires a continuously measured, deployment-specific availability distribution. This selection is written assuming a Carbonite-style reintegration trigger sits beneath it; a threshold-based trigger instead would multiply this section's repair-traffic figures by roughly the 2x-versus-1.44x ratio `CHUN-NSDI-06` measures between the two policies.
- A loss-detection mechanism must exist to tell a node when to invoke repair at all; the corpus's Proofs of Retrievability / Provable Data Possession literature (`ATENIESE-CCS-07`) supplies one design for this (constant-size challenge-response, independent of file size) but is a separate component from repair itself and is not evaluated here for composition with LRC specifically.

### What it costs and where it fails

Storage overhead: 1.33x-1.6x depending on parameters, against 1.4x-2x for matched-reliability plain Reed-Solomon and 2x-16x for replication at comparable redundancy targets (`HUANG-ATC-12`, `WILKINSON-STORJ-18`, `BHAGWAN-NSDI-04`). Repair traffic: 41-52% of plain Reed-Solomon's bytes moved, measured live on a 51-node EC2 Hadoop cluster (`SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13`); reconstruction cost cut from 6 fragments to 3 at matched overhead, measured on Azure production infrastructure (`HUANG-ATC-12`). Reliability, from `SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13`'s Markov model at N=3000 nodes, 30 PB cluster data, 4-year mean disk lifetime: LRC(10,6,5) reaches a Mean Time to Data Loss the model computes as "2 more zeros" than plain RS(10,4) — this model assumes independent node failures at an annual, managed-datacenter rate, not volunteer-scale churn (see below).

It fails, in the sense of falling back to full-cost repair, whenever a local group loses more members simultaneously than its local parity covers; `SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13`'s own Markov model computes the resulting light-versus-heavy-decoder invocation probability, but only under that managed-datacenter failure-rate assumption. It fails to defend against a Byzantine group member at all without the content-hash addition stated above as a requirement this selection needs, since Reed-Solomon-decode-plus-XOR is an erasure-correcting operation, not an error-correcting one, and silently produces an incorrect result when combined with corrupted-but-present input rather than a detected absence. It fails to reduce bandwidth if the group-membership placement collapses fault domains, since the reliability model's independence assumption is then violated without the system detecting the violation (`HUANG-ATC-12`).

### What the corpus does not settle

No retrieved source measures repair traffic, in bytes moved, for any coded repair mechanism — LRC included — operating on a population whose churn resembles Storj's measured 9.6% monthly node turnover or Gnutella's higher continuous turnover; this extends the corpus's own existing open-problem finding for regenerating codes specifically (`registry/open-problems/volunteer-repair-economics.md`) to the LRC family this selection picks, since Azure and Facebook's deployments are both managed datacenters, not open participant pools. The Markov reliability models both production LRC papers rely on (`HUANG-ATC-12`, `SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13`) assume independent failures at an annual per-disk rate; nothing in the corpus recomputes those models' light/heavy-decoder invocation probability under a churn rate two to three orders of magnitude higher.

Whether the content-hash verification this selection requires (stated above) is sufficient against a helper that withholds a correct response rather than substituting a wrong one — a liveness attack, not a correctness attack — is not evaluated by any retrieved source; `LI-IWQOS-23-STORJ`'s reputation-manipulation finding shows the layer that decides which nodes get asked is separately attackable, independent of whether a given response can be verified once received.

The finite-field size and local-parity coefficient-selection procedure LRC repair needs is stated by `SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13` to be "the main theoretical challenge" of the construction and deferred to an appendix the retrieved text does not include as extracted content; a builder needs the concrete field size and coefficients, not the paper's claim that a valid choice exists.

Whether RedStuff's verified-recovery construction, the corpus's only evidence that Byzantine-tolerant coded repair with sub-linear bandwidth is achievable at all, would sustain its measured recovery durations (17-64 hours per multi-shard event, on committee nodes with median 55 TB capacity and 1 Gbps bandwidth) under a churn rate resembling Storj's or Gnutella's is not addressed by `DANEZIS-WALRUS-25` or any other retrieved source. Whether a lighter-weight verification scheme than a full vector commitment — one closing only the gap this selection's local-group repair already avoids by construction, rather than the harder gap regenerating-code and 2D-coding repair face — has been published anywhere is a literature question this pass did not separately search.
