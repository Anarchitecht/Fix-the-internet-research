### Selection

Compaction over a verifiable key directory with threshold-witness quorum-certificate signing (Parakeet, `MALVAI-NDSS-23`) is selected.

Against the CONIKS-family chained-snapshot mechanisms audited by the key owner (CONIKS `MELARA-USENIXSEC-15`, Merkle² `HU-SP-21`, SEEMless `CHASE-CCS-19`): all three place the cross-party equivocation check on a population of auditors the mechanism does not itself create. `MELARA-USENIXSEC-15` states this directly: the scheme "provides no mechanism to force that gossip to happen, only the format of the evidence once it does." `CHASE-CCS-19` states the equivalent gap explicitly as an out-of-scope dependency: it "explicitly leaves that mechanism outside SEEMless itself," naming a blockchain post or a gossip protocol as external options neither built nor measured in that paper. `HU-SP-21` inherits the same gap; its contribution is bounding what an eventual auditor's own re-check costs, not supplying the auditor. An entry that nobody in this population happens to check is served with a fully valid-looking authentication path to every individual requester, each of whom sees no local anomaly, and the substitution stands until two specific viewers who were shown different values compare notes or a volunteer auditor finds the fork. Parakeet closes this by making the check itself a required step of every round: a quorum-certificate cannot exist for an epoch unless at least 2f+1 of 3f+1 witnesses verified an append-only audit proof against the previous digest and voted, at a measured 68.70 ms per verification and 0.02 ms per vote on ordinary server hardware (`MALVAI-NDSS-23`), cheap enough to run on every round rather than depend on whether some volunteer happened to check.

Against periodic root-digest posting to a public blockchain (EthIKS, Catena): neither paper has a full-text entry in this corpus, so no measured cost or proof for this specific variant can be cited as evidence rather than as the citing papers' own summary judgment. `CHASE-CCS-19`'s and `MALVAI-NDSS-23`'s bibliography annotations both characterize per-operation blockchain interaction as reaching an infeasible or centralization-reintroducing cost at scale, but these are secondhand characterizations attributed to the citing paper, not independently checked figures, and are recorded here as such rather than as a settled comparison.

Against a full on-chain binding with no separate directory (Namecoin-style, `KALODNER-WEIS-15`): this variant does supply universal, non-optional auditing — any full node validates every committed binding as a side effect of following consensus, which is a stronger property than any of the other candidates offer on paper. But the property it substitutes for auditing is trust in whoever currently holds a majority of block-production power, and `KALODNER-WEIS-15` measures that this specific deployment held roughly one-third of Bitcoin's hash rate, explicitly noting "a sufficiently large Bitcoin mining pool could still carry [a 51% attack] out." `AMBATI-SACMAT-26`'s survey of 47 blockchain-anchored identity systems finds this single-chain dependence unresolved across the field (0 of 12 real deployments achieve full self-sovereignty on all five surveyed dimensions), and states no cross-ledger interoperability standard exists to mitigate it. A design whose stated goal is that identity cannot be captured by any company should not make key-substitution detection depend on which entity currently controls the largest share of one chain's block production; Parakeet's witness quorum is a smaller, purpose-built, explicitly Byzantine-fault-tolerant committee (3f+1, tolerating f), not a general-purpose mining/staking majority, and its size and membership are a parameter the surrounding system controls rather than a majority contest against a much larger external chain's total hash power.

### What this selection requires from the rest of the system

- A witness committee of size 3f+1, reachable over an asynchronous network by both the identity provider and every client, with the assumption that no more than f witnesses are simultaneously Byzantine in any given round; the composition/governance layer of the wider architecture must supply the process that selects and rotates this committee's membership without letting one operator control 2f+1 of it — `MALVAI-NDSS-23` does not itself specify this process.
- An identity or naming component that publishes username-to-key updates in discrete rounds (epochs) to a verifiable key directory instantiated as an ordered zero-knowledge set over a compressed Merkle Patricia Trie, so that a compact per-round digest exists for the witnesses to check — `MALVAI-NDSS-23`.
- Persistent storage at the identity provider with fast random writes at the scale of the benchmarked prototype (RocksDB or MySQL); the measured storage grows linearly with registered users (≈4.5 GB at 5 million users), not with epochs elapsed — `MALVAI-NDSS-23`.
- Durable private signing keys held by the identity provider and by each witness, with identity-provider key compromise explicitly out of scope: this mechanism detects equivocation given an honest witness majority, it does not prevent a compromised provider's data from looking internally consistent before compromise is discovered by some other means — `MALVAI-NDSS-23`.
- A residual per-user obligation the client software must schedule on its own: re-querying the user's own key history at least once between any tombstone epoch and the following deletion epoch, or a wrongful deletion of that specific user's binding goes undetected — `MALVAI-NDSS-23`.
- Bounded clock drift among witnesses sufficient for the paper's stated f-fault-tolerance argument; the paper references this assumption without a numeric bound, so the transport/time-synchronization component must supply a bound this corpus does not derive.
- A client-reachability path to a quorum of 2f+1 witnesses (not merely to the identity provider), so whatever transport and NAT-traversal mechanism the wider architecture selects must let ordinary clients reach that quorum, not only reach the provider.

### What it costs and where it fails

Verify notification 68.70 ms mean (1.50 ms std, single core, AWS t3.medium witness machine, 4-witness committee, notification batch of 1,024 updates at 64 bytes each, 10-run mean); create vote 0.02 ms mean; verify certificate 0.16 ms mean (certificate = 2f+1 = 3 votes for f=1 on a 4-witness committee); end-to-end maximum throughput ≈800 updates/s at latency under 3.5 s on a WAN testbed spanning 5 AWS regions with 10 honest witnesses and batch size 1,024; throughput is flat across 10, 20, and 50 witnesses and unaffected by 1 or 3 crashed witnesses out of 10 — all `MALVAI-NDSS-23`. VKD storage measured at ≈4.5 GB at 5 million users, growing linearly; a projection (not a direct measurement) of ≈850 GB at 1 billion users — `MALVAI-NDSS-23`.

It stops working, for the non-equivocation guarantee specifically, once fewer than 2f+1 of the 3f+1 witnesses are honest in a given round; the paper states the soundness proof is void with zero honest auditors and does not report a measured degradation curve between that point and full quorum, because the reported crash-fault benchmarks (0, 1, 3 crashes out of 10) all stayed within the tolerable range and no Byzantine-fault benchmark was run. It stops protecting a specific user's binding against wrongful deletion whenever that user does not re-query their own key history between a tombstone epoch and the following deletion epoch. It has no answer in the retrieved text for the "right to be forgotten" tension the paper itself names: the append-only base construction beneath compaction conflicts with a regulatory requirement for full account-data deletion.

### What the corpus does not settle

No numeric bound for the required clock-drift tolerance among witnesses; `MALVAI-NDSS-23` states the assumption exists without a stated value. No measured behavior under actively Byzantine (as opposed to crash-faulty) witnesses; the authors call this "still an open question" and report only crash-fault results. No description in this corpus of a witness-selection or witness-rotation process that itself resists capture by a single operator — this is exactly the composition question the architecture's "cannot be captured by any company" goal raises, and neither `MALVAI-NDSS-23` nor any other entry in this corpus answers it. The 850 GB storage figure at 1-billion-user scale is an extrapolation from a 5-million-user measurement, not a direct measurement. No entry in this corpus reports a direct, matched-configuration benchmark comparing Parakeet against Merkle²; `MALVAI-NDSS-23` names Merkle² only in its bibliography. Full-text evidence for the periodic blockchain-digest-posting family (EthIKS, Catena) was not retrieved anywhere in this corpus; every claim about that family's cost above is a secondhand characterization from a paper that cites it, not an independently checked figure, and a person choosing between that family and the selection here would need those two papers' own text before the comparison in this document could be treated as settled rather than provisional.
