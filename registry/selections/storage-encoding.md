## Storage encoding: plain Reed-Solomon erasure coding is selected, and the churn regime that decides this is the high-turnover regime, not the low-churn regime most repair-cost-reducing codes were measured in

Storage encoding turns D bytes of unique data a person stores into a larger set of pieces spread across independently operated holders, so the data survives some holders departing permanently, without needing every original holder to remain reachable. Every candidate below is compared on three figures the brief requires for each: the storage-overhead ratio (total bytes stored across all holders, divided by D), the reconstruction threshold (how many holders must respond to recover the data in full), and the count of holders that must be live simultaneously to complete a single-piece repair — a number that is not always equal to the reconstruction threshold, and the gap between the two is where several candidates fail under churn.

Two entries in the corpus reach opposite feasibility conclusions from what looks like the same question, and the gap is a modeled-population difference, not a numeric disagreement: `BLAKE-HOTOS-03` derives a bandwidth bound from an original crawl of ~33,000 Gnutella hosts (April 2003; the availability and permanent-failure rates previously stated here as a=0.38 and 30%/day are NOT this paper's figures — they are `DIMAKIS-TIT-10`'s, for a different Gnutella trace of 1,846 nodes taken May 2001 over a 2.5-day window. `BLAKE-HOTOS-03` reports its own parameters only as swept curves, not as single values) and states storing 1 TB of unique data at high availability is "hopeless" with that population's participation and cable-modem bandwidth. `CHUN-NSDI-06` (Carbonite) measures a 632-host, one-year PlanetLab trace (mean disk lifetime 2.23 years and sustainability ratio θ≈6.85 are this paper's own figures; the a=0.97 previously stated here is NOT — it is `DIMAKIS-TIT-10`'s figure for a separate 303-node PlanetLab trace. `CHUN-NSDI-06` reports a=0.88, and only for a separate 5-year synthetic trace) and reports its algorithm uses only 44% more traffic than an oracle that repairs solely on true disk failure. `CHUN-NSDI-06` itself states the reason for the different conclusion: it analyzes "a relatively stable system membership where data loss is driven by disk failure," not Gnutella's continual membership turnover. This architecture recruits arbitrary, independently operated participants rather than institutionally hosted or staked ones (BRIEF §1); the measured population closest to that description in the corpus is `ANDERSON-CCGRID-06`'s SETI@home/BOINC volunteer pool, mean host lifetime 89.5 days — two orders of magnitude shorter than PlanetLab's 2.23-year mean disk lifetime, and closer in character to Gnutella's turnover than to PlanetLab's stability. The applicable regime is therefore `BLAKE-HOTOS-03`'s regime, not `CHUN-NSDI-06`'s. This resolution matters directly for candidate selection below: the one place a regenerating code was tested against a Gnutella-scale trace (`DIMAKIS-TIT-10`), its measured advantage over a simpler design nearly disappears, and every locally-repairable-code and regenerating-code repair-cost figure in the corpus otherwise comes from a data-center or research-testbed population, never a volunteer-turnover one.

### Selection

Plain Reed-Solomon (n,k) MDS erasure coding is selected as the storage-encoding mechanism, over whole-copy replication, over minimum-storage and minimum-bandwidth regenerating codes, over locally repairable codes, over convertible codes, and over rateless codes.

Against whole-copy replication: every fixed-parameter comparison in the corpus that holds durability or storage overhead constant favors plain coding, by a wide and consistent margin — 11× less bandwidth, storage, and disk seeks at matched MTTF [`WEATHERSPOON-IPTPS-02`], 25× versus 4.5× overhead at a matched 10⁻¹² durability target [`DANEZIS-WALRUS-25`], and two orders of magnitude higher MTTF at the same fault-domain population in a production system [`HUANG-ATC-12`]. Replication's only structural advantage — no field arithmetic, verification against a raw byte-for-byte copy — is not decisive: RS decode cost is measured at 13.2 μs per 4 KB fragment, far below fragment-transfer time, so the corpus gives no evidence that replication's simplicity offsets its bandwidth and storage cost [`HUANG-ATC-12`].

Against minimum-storage regenerating (MSR) codes: `RASHMI-TIT-11`'s exact-repair construction needs d≥2k-2 helper nodes simultaneously reachable to realize its bandwidth advantage, a stricter simultaneous-liveness requirement than plain RS's k, and the predecessor OMMDS construction needs n-1 — nearly every remaining holder live at once [`DIMAKIS-TIT-10`]. Under the low-churn PlanetLab-like trace where regenerating codes were measured to help, high simultaneous reachability is cheap (a=0.97); under the applicable high-churn regime this document establishes above, it is exactly the resource that is scarce. No paper in the corpus measures MSR repair bandwidth against any trace resembling that regime — `RASHMI-TIT-11` is theory only, and `DIMAKIS-TIT-10`'s own Gnutella-trace result for the related MBR construction shows the family's advantage evaporating in that regime.

Against minimum-bandwidth regenerating (MBR) codes / RC: this candidate needs only d=k live helpers, matching plain RS's threshold, and its proved bandwidth reduction (84% below naive download, up to 58× less bandwidth at matched unavailability) is real under the traces tested [`DIMAKIS-TIT-10`]. But every trace on which that reduction was measured to hold is PlanetLab-, Microsoft-desktop-, or Skype-superpeer-scale churn; on the paper's own Gnutella-scale trace — the regime this document establishes as applicable — the construction "can be very slightly worse than Hybrid" [`DIMAKIS-TIT-10`]. Selecting MBR regenerating codes for this architecture would mean relying on a measured advantage from the wrong regime and a same-paper result from the right regime showing that advantage gone; plain RS carries no such regime-mismatch risk because its repair-bandwidth cost is not measured as an improvement over some baseline in the first place, only as a fixed multiple of object size.

Against locally repairable codes: every repair-cost reduction measured for this family — 50% fewer reads, 41%-52% of RS's repair bytes, 25%-45% faster repair [`HUANG-ATC-12`, `SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13`] — comes from Windows Azure Storage, HDFS-Xorbas on Amazon EC2, or Facebook's production Hadoop cluster: institutionally hosted nodes on 1 Gbps links, not volunteer participants. The precondition those figures rest on, "more than 98% of failure events are single-chunk" [`CHENG-TOS25-ECSURVEY`], is itself measured only in that Facebook population; nothing in the corpus establishes whether volunteer-scale churn produces the same single-fragment-dominant failure pattern or a more correlated one, and `CHENG-TOS25-ECSURVEY`'s own literature review — the field's current systematization — cites no peer-to-peer or volunteer-churn repair measurement newer than 2005. Adopting LRC's added complexity (group-membership tracking, fault-domain placement, a code that is not fully MDS and fails 14% of 4-simultaneous-failure patterns even in its measured regime [`HUANG-ATC-12`]) is not supported by evidence in the regime this architecture occupies, whereas plain RS's measured behavior does not depend on that same single-fragment-dominant assumption.

Against convertible codes: this family reduces the bandwidth of changing an already-deployed code's redundancy parameters, not the storage-overhead or reconstruction-threshold of the encoding itself, and every retrieved construction is theory only — no implementation, simulation, or deployment measurement exists for any of the four papers [`MATURANA-TIT-22`, `MATURANA-ISIT-23`, `KONG-TIT-24`, `SHI-ARXIV-26`]. More directly disqualifying for an open network: every construction requires the initial and final code parameters to be fixed and paired in advance — `MATURANA-TIT-22` states this explicitly as a precondition for its own access-optimal guarantee, and `KONG-TIT-24` states its construction "is not parameter-agnostic." A network recruiting arbitrary, unpredictable volunteer participants cannot commit in advance to the specific future redundancy target this family's access-cost optimality depends on, so this is not a candidate for the base encoding at all; it is at best a future add-on if a specific, planned re-parameterization becomes needed.

Against rateless codes: this family was designed for point-to-multipoint object delivery over a lossy channel, where a receiver decides unilaterally how many symbols to collect — not for fixed-membership storage among specific holders whose individual departure has to be repaired. The corpus gives it no built-in defense against a corrupted or substituted symbol [`LUBY-RFC-11`, `SHOKROLLAHI-TIT-06`], the same gap plain RS has, but unlike plain RS it has no fixed reconstruction threshold or holder-liveness count to reason about at all, which makes composing it with a repair-scheduling or placement mechanism that needs to know "how many holders must be live" (the number this document's whole comparison runs on) structurally awkward rather than merely unmeasured. The corpus's one deployment data point for this family in a decentralized-storage role is negative: `DANEZIS-WALRUS-25`'s production system moved away from RaptorQ to Reed-Solomon after a reported vulnerability, the opposite direction from adoption.

### What this selection requires from the rest of the system

- A placement mechanism that assigns n fragment-holders per stripe of an object and can locate at least k of them on demand, at both read time and repair time; the encoding itself specifies no discovery mechanism [`WEATHERSPOON-IPTPS-02` requires fragments "placed on independently, uniformly randomly selected disks" for its durability figures to hold; `DIMAKIS-TIT-10` states the same discovery step is assumed, not supplied].
- A per-fragment integrity mechanism independent of the code: RS decoding does not, by itself, detect a fragment a dishonest holder has substituted. `WILKINSON-STORJ-18` states explicitly that Reed-Solomon decoding alone is insufficient to confirm correctness when only the minimum k fragments are downloaded, and requires a separate per-piece hash validated against the object's metadata pointer. `DANEZIS-WALRUS-25`'s Byzantine-tolerant variant instead verifies each returned symbol against a vector commitment (a Merkle tree in its deployment). Whichever naming or identity component a builder chooses elsewhere for this architecture must supply that per-fragment commitment or hash.
- A repair-trigger mechanism that distinguishes transient unreachability from permanent departure, since the encoding itself carries no notion of a membership timeout. `BLAKE-HOTOS-03`'s own worst-case bandwidth figure improves roughly 30× once transient downtime is distinguished from departure at a well-chosen timeout τ, and `CHUN-NSDI-06`'s reintegration rule (readmitting a returned replica rather than discarding it) is what keeps its measured overhead near an oracle's on its own trace. This decision sits in a repair/maintenance component this document does not select, not in the encoding.
- Field arithmetic (Galois-field operations) at every fragment-producing and reconstructing participant — a lower bar than the regenerating-code candidates, which additionally need every helper node to compute and transmit a linear combination of stored sub-symbols during repair, not merely serve a stored fragment unmodified [`CHENG-TOS25-ECSURVEY`].
- Independent, or at least uncorrelated, failure across the n holders of one stripe for the durability figures above to hold; every cited comparison assumes this (`HUANG-ATC-12`'s fault-domain placement requirement; `WEATHERSPOON-IPTPS-02`'s independent-disk assumption). No entry in the corpus measures how correlated volunteer-participant churn actually is (shared ISP outages, shared software-version bugs, coordinated departure events), so the placement mechanism's own diversity guarantee — not measured here — is a precondition this selection assumes rather than one it establishes.

### What it costs and where it fails

The storage-overhead ratio n/k is a design choice, not a value this corpus derives; production data-center deployments cluster it between 1.18× and 1.50× [`CHENG-TOS25-ECSURVEY`], but that figure reflects independent-disk-failure populations, not volunteer churn, and no entry in the corpus states what ratio a volunteer-churn population needs for a stated durability target. The one real (not simulated) full-repair-event bandwidth figure for plain RS in the corpus — 486.6 GB read to repair 369 lost blocks (1.318 GB/block), in 26 minutes [`SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13`] — comes from a Facebook production cluster, not a volunteer population.

The encoding fails whenever fewer than k of a stripe's n holders are simultaneously reachable. `RODRIGUES-IPTPS-05`'s own Overnet-trace result is the closest evidence in the corpus to a direct answer for the applicable regime: even after applying erasure coding and reducing bandwidth substantially relative to replication, the resulting maintenance bandwidth the trace's own paper computes for four-nines availability is stated by the authors themselves as unsustainable for home users. Plain RS narrows the gap `BLAKE-HOTOS-03` identifies; it does not close it. Nothing in the corpus establishes a redundancy level and repair-trigger policy at which four-or-more-nines durability becomes sustainable for a genuinely open, volunteer-recruited population — this is the same gap the corpus's own `volunteer-repair-economics` open-problem entry documents in full.

### What the corpus does not settle

No paper in the corpus measures repair-traffic bytes for any erasure-code family — plain RS, MSR, MBR, or LRC — on a population with volunteer-scale churn. The closest measured churn rate to "volunteer" is Storj's live network, 9.6% average monthly node turnover across a 16-month, 32,881-node crawl spanning 122 countries [`LI-IWQOS-23-STORJ`], but that paper reports no repair-byte count at all, and Storj's own repair-bandwidth table is a 10,000-node Monte Carlo simulation under an assumed constant monthly loss rate, not measured live traffic [`WILKINSON-STORJ-18`]. Closing this gap requires either instrumenting a live plain-RS deployment recruiting genuinely open participants and reporting repair bytes moved, or re-running `WEATHERSPOON-IPTPS-02`'s or `DIMAKIS-TIT-10`'s cost model against a churn trace collected after 2020, neither of which any retrieved source has done.

Given that gap, the specific (n,k) parameters a real volunteer deployment should target — the overhead-versus-repair-frequency tradeoff point — is not derivable from this corpus. A builder choosing a value anyway is making a labelled pick, not reporting a derived or measured figure, and should record it as such.

Whether adding LRC-style local parity groups on top of the selected RS code would help or hurt under volunteer churn is unmeasured, not merely unmeasured-but-probably-fine: the 50% and 41%-52% repair-cost reductions both rest on "more than 98% of failures are single-chunk" [`CHENG-TOS25-ECSURVEY`, citing a Facebook measurement], a property that has never been checked against a population where correlated departure (shared outages, coordinated client-version bugs, a mass-exit event) may be common rather than rare.

The interaction between the selected encoding and a verified-retrievability layer (a proof that a holder actually stores the fragment it claims to, as opposed to merely having once received it) is a separate component this document does not score. `JUELS-CCS-07` and `SHACHAM-ASIACRYPT-08` give only proved asymptotic bounds, no deployment measurement, for the proof-of-retrievability constructions that compose with an erasure-encoded object; what added bandwidth and latency that composition costs at volunteer scale is not established here.

Whether the Byzantine-tolerant verification RS needs against a substituting holder (a per-fragment hash, or a vector commitment as in `DANEZIS-WALRUS-25`) changes the repair-bandwidth or storage-overhead figures reported above for plain RS is not stated by any of the plain-RS-measuring papers in this corpus (`WEATHERSPOON-IPTPS-02`, `RODRIGUES-IPTPS-05`, `SATHIAMOORTHY-VLDB-13`'s RS baseline) — all of them assume a crash-fault, not Byzantine, failure model for the encoding layer itself, leaving the added cost of Byzantine-tolerant verification on top of plain RS an open figure for this architecture specifically, since it targets a permissionless, uncoordinated participant population where a Byzantine holder cannot be ruled out by assumption.


### Correction, from the claims audit

Three claims in this selection were corrected after every cited figure was traced back to the entry
it cites. Two availability figures stated above were attributed to the wrong papers: `a=0.38` with a
30 percent daily permanent-failure rate, and `a=0.97`, both belong to `DIMAKIS-TIT-10` and describe
different traces than the ones this document attributed them to. The whole-copy-replication row's
figures are analytical rather than measured, and were laid out in the same table as
production-measured rows without that distinction.

**The selection does not change.** The regime argument rests on host lifetime, not on those
availability figures: `ANDERSON-CCGRID-06` measures a volunteer pool at a mean host lifetime of 89.5
days and `CHUN-NSDI-06` measures PlanetLab at a mean disk lifetime of 2.23 years, and both of those
figures check out against their own entries. The two orders of magnitude between them is what places
this architecture in the high-turnover regime, and that is what decides against every
repair-cost-reducing code below.
