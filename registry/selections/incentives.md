## Incentives: storage-retrievability proofs settle payment for capacity; a per-connection reciprocation rule, not a credit ledger, settles bandwidth — and no candidate in the corpus does both

### Selection

Two different mechanisms are selected, one per resource, because the corpus shows no single candidate covering both, and one entry in the corpus states the structural reason directly: Samsara's own authors, having built a placeholder-claim mechanism for storage, write that they "cannot see how to build an equivalent placeholder for a renewable resource such as bandwidth," because monitoring bandwidth's use would itself consume it (`COX-SOSP-03`). Storage is a persistent, auditable state a random challenge can interrogate at any later moment; bandwidth exists only during the transfer that consumes it and must be verified in the moment or not at all.

For storage capacity, the selection is payment verified by a storage-retrievability proof: a blockchain-recorded transaction released automatically when a host answers a randomly timed challenge, built on the Shacham-Waters compact proof-of-retrievability construction and enforced the way Sia enforces it — reading only public data (`VORICK-SIA-14`, `SHACHAM-ASIACRYPT-08`).

For bandwidth, the selection is reciprocal exchange refined toward a proportional-share auction (PropShare) rather than left as classic discretized tit-for-tat: each peer returns upload bandwidth to its neighbors in proportion to what each neighbor sent it in the prior round, rather than splitting capacity equally among a fixed number of top bidders (`LEVIN-SIGCOMM-08`). This distinction matters because classic tit-for-tat is exactly what a strategic client exploits: BitTyrant extracts a median 1.72x download-rate gain from BitTorrent's discretized, equal-split reward, because the top (s-1) bidders at any peer all receive an identical 1/s share regardless of how much more the highest of them contributed, so minimally outbidding the current floor is a free strategy (`PIATEK-NSDI-07`, `LEVIN-SIGCOMM-08`). PropShare closes this by construction — the paper proves algebraically that a fixed total contribution split across any number of Sybil identities returns the identical aggregate bandwidth as one identity would have received, and measures a deviating peer's best-response gain at under 1% of download speed even though that peer's optimal contribution differs from the proportional allocation by roughly 30% (`LEVIN-SIGCOMM-08`).

Against payment verified by a bandwidth-forwarding proof (TorCoin) or a witnessed-capacity proof (Proof of Backhaul) as the bandwidth mechanism: Proof of Backhaul is the corpus's strongest evidence that verifying bandwidth cheaply is possible at all — under 5% error using about 3.5 MB of challenge traffic with 6 or more witnessing challengers over a 100 ms window, accurate to under 10% error up to 1,000 Mbps (`SHENG-NDSS-24`) — but it measures a node's aggregate link capacity, not which specific peer benefited from a specific transfer, so it does not by itself settle a payment between two communicating peers; it needs a witnessing group of otherwise-uninvolved challengers as an added party the reciprocal-exchange candidate does not need. TorCoin attempts exactly the missing connection, minting currency from a proof that one specific circuit forwarded traffic, at a stated approximately-5% overhead (`GHOSH-HOTPETS-14`) — but the authors label that figure "Preliminary Results" from a Python-Twisted message-passing simulation, not a measurement of packets moving between real relays, so it does not clear the bar this corpus sets for treating a number as a measured cost. Reciprocal exchange's own overhead figure — under 0.1% of bandwidth for piece announcement — is drawn from real deployments (`COHEN-IPTPS-03`), which the bandwidth-forwarding-proof evidence does not yet reach.

Against credit balances (KARMA, Dandelion) for either resource: both variants leave resource pricing to a decision the corpus does not supply. KARMA states the resource-pricing mechanism is "left entirely to the application," and its own second-order incentive problem — bank-set members have no built-in reason to perform bank-node work honestly — is presented with a proposed fix the authors flag as risking "a runaway chain reaction of compensatory transactions" (`VISHNUMURTHY-P2PECON-03`); a storage-retrievability proof instead settles price at contract formation and releases payment automatically on a passed challenge, with no discretionary gap (`VORICK-SIA-14`). Dandelion demonstrates the throughput a centralized credit clearinghouse can sustain — about 1,200 operations per second on one commodity server (`SIRIVIANOS-USENIXATC-07`) — but its own non-manipulability proof requires that single server to be a mandatory party to every chunk exchange, which the authors describe as a deliberate trade: "we trade scalability for the ability to provide robust incentives" (same source). Reciprocal exchange needs no such third party in the transfer path (`COHEN-IPTPS-03`), and a storage-retrievability proof's third party is a ledger recording public data, not a continuously available clearinghouse authenticating every individual exchange.

Against symmetric barter without a proof (Samsara) for storage: Samsara enforces that a consumer holds an equal-sized claim but makes no retrievability claim about the original object at all — the authors state the scheme "does not stop a node that promises to store data and then immediately discards it" (`COX-SOSP-03`). A storage-retrievability proof directly answers the question Samsara's authors say they do not address: the extraction guarantee recovers a rho-fraction of the file from any server passing an epsilon-fraction of challenges (`SHACHAM-ASIACRYPT-08`).

Against voluntary contribution for either resource: under no enforced reciprocity, 66-69% of a measured Gnutella population shared zero files and the top 1% of hosts answered 47% of query-response traffic, a concentration the source paper itself describes as producing "a rather centralized server" (`ADAR-FM-00`). Both selected mechanisms replace this by making non-contribution costly rather than merely hoped against: an unmet storage-proof challenge window routes contract funds to an unspendable address with no client action required (`VORICK-SIA-14`), and reciprocal exchange withholds upload bandwidth from a non-reciprocating peer by construction (`COHEN-IPTPS-03`).

### What this selection requires from the rest of the system

Storage-proof payment:
- A blockchain or equivalent ledger that supplies per-challenge-window randomness the prover cannot bias in advance, and that executes the payment transaction automatically on a valid proof while redirecting funds on a missed window — no client-side verification action is needed (`VORICK-SIA-14`).
- A content-addressed storage and transfer layer beneath the proof protocol, since Get/Put move the actual bytes outside the proof exchange (`BENET-FILECOIN-17`).
- An erasure code whose rate rho, whose challenge-index count l, and whose coefficient set B are chosen jointly, not independently, so that the extraction bound epsilon-omega stays positive and non-negligible (`SHACHAM-ASIACRYPT-08`).
- A separate payment-channel primitive to release funds per delivered part during retrieval, distinct from the storage-proof settlement, since a storage proof certifies continued possession, not willingness to transfer (`BENET-FILECOIN-17`).
- A multi-host placement or erasure-coding layer above the single-host contract: Sia's own paper states "storing data on a single untrusted host guarantees little in the way of availability, bandwidth, or general quality of service" (`VORICK-SIA-14`).

Reciprocal exchange for bandwidth:
- A peer-discovery channel (tracker, DHT, or both) able to return a candidate set larger and more frequently refreshed than a default 50-100-peer neighborhood, since extracting reciprocation from high-capacity peers needs hundreds of candidate connections (`PIATEK-NSDI-07`).
- A per-piece or per-chunk integrity check that gates whether a peer is willing to advertise a piece as held, since that same held-piece signal feeds both reciprocation accounting and rarest-first piece selection (`COHEN-IPTPS-03`).
- Bidirectional connections between exchanging peers, since choking withholds only the upload direction and leaves the same connection able to resume without renegotiation (`COHEN-IPTPS-03`).
- Each peer's own observation of how much bandwidth a given neighbor sent it in the prior round or rounds — no observation of the neighbor's total capacity or its bids to other peers (`LEVIN-SIGCOMM-08`).
- Acceptance of an unconditional, low-cost bootstrap allocation for newly joined peers with nothing yet to reciprocate; this is the same allocation BitThief and the large-view exploit consume, and the corpus states no closed defense for it (`LOCHER-HOTNETS-06`).

### What it costs and where it fails

Storage-proof payment costs 20 bytes per challenge and 40 bytes per response at 80-bit security for the publicly-verifiable construction, plus a 2x storage overhead beyond the erasure code's own expansion at one sector per block, reducible to (1+1/s)x at the cost of a (1+s)x longer response (`SHACHAM-ASIACRYPT-08`); Filecoin's design target of a 10-100x sealing-time slowdown relative to the challenge-response step is a stated design parameter, not a measured benchmark, since the paper reports no implementation figures at all (`BENET-FILECOIN-17`). It stops working when the challenge randomness can be biased by the party being audited — Sia states an attacker controlling 50% of block-producing power can manipulate roughly 50% of challenges (`VORICK-SIA-14`) — or when the underlying proof construction certifies only an aggregate property across many blocks rather than individual-block possession, which Shacham and Waters' own attack on a prior scheme (E-PDP) exploits to cheat with near-9% success while storing no more than an honest host (`SHACHAM-ASIACRYPT-08`).

Reciprocal exchange costs under 0.1% of bandwidth for piece announcement in real deployments (`COHEN-IPTPS-03`); a strategic client extracts a median 1.72x download-rate gain over an unmodified reference client (a 1 Mb/s-class client's median gain is 70%) across 114 real swarms of 300-800 peers, both clients capped at 128 KB/s upload (`PIATEK-NSDI-07`), and enlarging the candidate-peer set to extract that gain raises the strategic client's own protocol overhead from 0.9% to 1.9% of received data (same source). PropShare reduces the corresponding best-response gain to under 1% of download speed (`LEVIN-SIGCOMM-08`). It stops working, in the sense of admitting a zero-contribution client, whenever a client targets the mechanism's unconditional bootstrap path instead of its reciprocation logic: BitThief completes every one of 7 tested torrents while uploading zero data, at roughly 4-5x the reference client's completion time in seeder-scarce swarms (`LOCHER-HOTNETS-06`), and the large-view exploit wins 12 of 15 public-torrent trials by holding a swarm view roughly 5x the compliant default (`SIRIVIANOS-IPTPS-07`). This failure is bounded, not unbounded: at a 60% free-rider population share in a controlled PlanetLab swarm, the large-view exploit's own advantage reverses and compliant peers finish faster than free-riders (`SIRIVIANOS-IPTPS-07`), and a community that verifies its own sharing ratio (TorrentLeech, contrasted against the open tracker Mininova in the same study) shows measurably more balanced per-peer contribution under the identical exploit (`LOCHER-HOTNETS-06`).

### What the corpus does not settle

No entry reports a measured, not simulated or design-stated, cost or throughput for a storage-retrievability proof running against a live, adversarial storage-provider population at production scale: Filecoin's paper reports zero implementation benchmarks (`BENET-FILECOIN-17`), and Sia's paper reports no deployment measurement of its storage-proof mechanism at all (`VORICK-SIA-14`).

No entry connects a witnessed bandwidth-capacity proof (Proof of Backhaul) to a per-transfer payment settlement measured on live traffic between two specific peers. TorCoin is the corpus's only attempt at that connection and its overhead figures are simulated, not measured (`GHOSH-HOTPETS-14`); whether a witnessed-challenge bandwidth proof can be composed with reciprocal exchange or with per-transfer payment at the low overhead Proof of Backhaul demonstrates for aggregate capacity measurement alone is not addressed by any retrieved source.

No entry proposes and measures a countermeasure to the large-view exploit against reciprocal exchange. Sirivianos et al.'s own synchronized-peer-assignment proposal — the countermeasure their paper describes — is stated as unimplemented, with its governing parameters (the interval duration, the post-interval connection delay, and the per-client allowed connection count) explicitly left as future work (`SIRIVIANOS-IPTPS-07`); a system relying on reciprocal exchange for bandwidth has no measured defense against this exploit to select from this corpus, only a bounded-failure observation (the exploit's advantage reverses once free-riders are a majority of the swarm).

No entry states how to jointly select a storage proof's erasure-code rate and its challenge-window frequency against a stated participant-churn rate for an open, volunteer-scale population. Storj's own erasure and audit parameters are stated as values that "may change in the future," chosen for that project's own measured churn (`LI-IWQOS-23-STORJ`), not derived from a general formula independent of a specific deployment's measured turnover.

No entry measures what fraction of a real population would supply nonzero storage or bandwidth once the specific free-riding paths this selection is exposed to (the unconditional bootstrap allocation, the large-view exploit) are closed by whatever defense is eventually built; the free-riding measurements in this corpus (`ADAR-FM-00`, `LOCHER-HOTNETS-06`, `SIRIVIANOS-IPTPS-07`) measure exploit success against the mechanism as currently specified, not the residual contributing population after a fix.

No entry measures Dandelion-style or KARMA-style credit balances at a scale or under an adversarial condition comparable to the two selected mechanisms' own evidence, so the comparison above rests on KARMA's closed-form probability and Dandelion's single-machine throughput ceiling, not on a deployed credit-balance system operating at the population scale Walrus, Storj, or real BitTorrent swarms are measured at.
